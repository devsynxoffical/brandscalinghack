Brand Scaling works as an ecommerce influencer marketing agency for DTC brands that want creator content to pay for itself. We run product seeding, paid creator deals and whitelisted partnership ads as one program, and we measure it the same way we measure Meta and TikTok media buying: by new-customer CAC and contribution margin.

Most influencer programs fail for a simple reason. A creator posts once, the post gets views, nobody can tell whether it sold anything, and the content is never used again. Our approach treats each creator as a source of ad creative and social proof, with the organic post as the first test rather than the whole deal.

What does an ecommerce influencer marketing agency do?

An ecommerce influencer marketing agency finds, briefs and pays creators to feature your product, then turns the best posts into paid ads through whitelisting or partnership ads. A good one secures usage rights up front and judges every creator on the new customers and CAC their content drives, not on likes or reach.

What an influencer program with us includes

The work covers four connected pieces. Each one feeds the next, so a creator who performs organically can move into paid amplification without renegotiating from scratch.

  • Product seeding: sending product to a wide list of relevant creators with no posting obligation, to find who genuinely likes it and who converts.
  • Paid creator partnerships: flat-fee or hybrid deals with creators whose audience matches your buyer, with a clear brief and deliverables.
  • Whitelisting and partnership ads: running the best creator posts as paid ads from the creator's handle on Meta, and as Spark Ads on TikTok.
  • Usage rights: written terms for how long and where you can run each video in paid ads, on your site and in email.

Influencer marketing vs affiliates vs UGC

These three get confused, and the confusion leads to the wrong budget in the wrong place. Influencer marketing pays for a creator's audience and credibility. An affiliate program pays a commission on tracked sales and suits creators who want an ongoing income from your product, which is a separate service we cover on our DTC affiliate program management page. UGC production pays creators for content only, filmed to a script for your own ad account, with no expectation that they post it. Our guide to scaling UGC production explains how that pipeline works.

In practice the three overlap. A seeded creator can become an affiliate, and a paid influencer post often becomes your best UGC-style ad once it is whitelisted. We plan them together so the same creator is not paid three times for the same video.

How we run a creator program

The process follows the same discipline as our paid media work: small tests first, scale only what the numbers support.

1. Creator selection

We shortlist creators by audience fit, content quality and how natural their sponsored posts look, not by follower count. Comment quality and the share of real buyer questions tell you more than a big number on the profile.

2. Seeding and first posts

A seeding round shows which creators post without being paid and which videos get saves, comments and link clicks. Those creators get the first paid offers.

3. Briefs that sell

Each brief gives the creator one angle, the problem the product solves, the proof points and the offer, while leaving the delivery in their own voice. Briefs that dictate every word produce content that looks like an ad and performs like one.

4. Whitelisting and paid amplification

The posts that show buying intent are run as partnership ads, with your spend behind the creator's name. This is where most of the measurable return sits, because you control the budget, the audience and the landing page.

Measuring creators on new-customer CAC

Views and engagement rate are easy to report and hard to bank. We set every creator up with a unique code and link, and once their content runs as an ad it is tracked inside the ad account like any other creative. The question for each creator is simple: what did it cost to acquire a new customer through their content, and is that below the CAC your margin allows?

To answer it, total cost includes the fee, product, shipping and the ad spend behind whitelisted posts. We compare that against the break-even CAC from your unit economics and against blended MER, because creator content also lifts branded search and direct traffic that discount codes never capture. Creators who clear the bar get repeat deals and longer usage rights. Creators who do not are thanked and dropped.

  • Unique discount code and tracked link per creator
  • Whitelisted posts reported as their own ads, on cost per purchase
  • All-in cost per creator: fee, product, shipping and amplification spend
  • Weekly read on new-customer CAC and blended MER

Usage rights, contracts and pricing models

Usage rights decide how much value you get from a creator deal. Without them, a video that converts brilliantly as an organic post cannot legally run in your ads. We agree the term (for example 30, 90 or 180 days), the channels, whether whitelisting access is included and whether you can edit the footage into new cuts, before any product ships.

Creator fees are usually flat per deliverable, a flat fee plus commission, or product only for seeding. Agency fees for running the program are typically a monthly retainer, sometimes combined with a share of the amplification budget. We quote after the Brand Growth Audit, once we know your category, margin and how many creators the program needs.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an influencer marketing agency cost for ecommerce?

Agency fees are usually a monthly retainer, sometimes with a share of the paid amplification budget, and creator fees are paid on top. Creator costs vary with audience size, deliverables and usage rights. Brand Scaling does not publish a price; we scope the program after a Brand Growth Audit, once we know your margin, category and the number of creators needed.

What is the difference between whitelisting and partnership ads?

Whitelisting is the general practice of running ads through a creator's identity with their permission. On Meta this is now done with partnership ads, where the ad shows both the creator's handle and your brand. On TikTok the equivalent is Spark Ads. In each case you control spend and targeting while the post keeps the creator's credibility.

Should I pay influencers a flat fee or commission?

Flat fees suit creators with proven conversion history and clear deliverables. Commission or affiliate deals suit creators who want ongoing income and lower your upfront risk. A hybrid, a smaller flat fee plus commission, often works best for first deals. Whatever the model, agree usage rights and whitelisting access in the same contract.

How do you measure influencer marketing ROI?

We add up the full cost of each creator, including fee, product, shipping and ad spend behind whitelisted posts, and divide by the new customers their content drives through codes, links and ad tracking. That gives a creator-level CAC to compare with your break-even CAC, alongside blended MER to catch sales codes miss.

Are micro influencers better than big influencers for DTC brands?

For most DTC brands, a group of smaller creators with engaged, relevant audiences gives more tests and more usable ad content than one large creator. Smaller creators are cheaper per video and more willing to grant usage rights. Larger creators can make sense for launches once smaller tests have proven the angle.

Do I need usage rights to run influencer content as ads?

Yes. Posting on their own profile does not give you the right to run a creator's video in your ads, on your site or in email. Usage rights should state the channels, the length of the term and whether you can edit the footage. Without them, your best-performing creator content cannot be scaled.
