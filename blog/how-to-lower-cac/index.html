How to Lower CAC: 7 Levers DTC Brands Can Pull (With the Math)

Direct Answer: To learn how to lower CAC, start with the formula: paid CAC equals cost per click divided by conversion rate. Lower it by improving creative so clicks get cheaper, raising conversion rate on your store, fixing tracking so the algorithm gets clean signals, consolidating audiences, and growing retention and referral so not every customer has to be bought.

CAC (customer acquisition cost): The average cost of winning one new customer: marketing spend divided by new customers in the same period. Blended CAC uses all spend and all new customers; paid CAC uses only ad spend and the customers those ads acquired.

CAC basics: blended versus paid

Before working out how to lower CAC, agree on which CAC you mean. Blended CAC divides all marketing spend by all new customers, including the ones who arrived through organic search, word of mouth or email. Paid CAC divides ad spend by the new customers the ads acquired. Blended CAC is the number that pays the bills; paid CAC is the one you can act on inside an ad account.

Track both monthly, and always next to contribution margin per first order and LTV. A CAC of $60 is excellent for a brand whose customers spend $400 over a year and a problem for one whose customers buy once at $45. The aim is not the lowest possible CAC; it is a CAC that pays back within a timeframe your cash flow can handle.

A worked example of CAC math

For paid traffic, CAC breaks down into two numbers you can improve separately. If you pay a cost per click (CPC) and a share of those clicks buy (conversion rate, CVR), then CAC = CPC / CVR. What the example shows

Two modest improvements, a 12% cheaper click and half a point more conversion rate, cut CAC by about 27%, from $50.00 to $36.67, with the same budget.

Now add margin. Say average order value is $70 and contribution margin after product cost, shipping and fees is 60%, so each first order contributes $42 before ads. At the baseline CAC of $50 the brand loses $8 on every first order. At $36.67 it makes $5.33. If a bundle lifts AOV to $84, contribution rises to $50.40 and the same CAC returns $13.73 per first order. Run your own numbers in the CAC calculator before you choose which lever to work on first.

Lever 1: creative

On Meta and TikTok, creative now does most of the targeting. Ads that stop the scroll and speak to a clear problem earn better engagement, which the platforms reward with cheaper delivery, and they attract people more likely to buy.

Creative affects both halves of the CAC formula. A sharper hook lowers cost per click, and an ad that sets honest expectations about price, use and results sends visitors who convert at a higher rate once they land.

The practical move is volume and variety. Test new angles, hooks and formats every week rather than small edits of one ad. For partner brands we produce 30 to 50+ new creatives a month, planned from a 5 angles x 3 hook styles x 3 formats matrix, because a steady supply of different ads is what keeps CPC from creeping up as audiences tire of the old ones.

  • Judge new ads on cost per purchase, not only click-through rate.
  • Refresh before fatigue shows: rising frequency and CPC are the early signs.
  • Match the landing page to the ad promise so cheaper clicks still convert.

Lever 2: offer and AOV

A stronger offer lowers CAC directly by converting more of the same visitors. Raising average order value does not change CAC itself, but it changes how much CAC you can afford, as the example showed.

Good offers add perceived value without cutting deep into margin: bundles of complementary products, quantity breaks on consumables, gifts with purchase above a threshold, and free shipping set just above your current AOV. Test them against a plain price, and judge by contribution margin per order, not just conversion rate.

Lever 3: conversion rate

Conversion rate improves CAC on every channel at once, which makes it the most underused lever. If your store converts 2.5% and you lift it to 3.0%, every ad, email and influencer post gets cheaper per customer.

Start where paid traffic lands. Speed up mobile load times, put the main benefit and price above the fold, show reviews with photos close to the add to cart button, answer shipping and return questions on the page, and remove steps from checkout. A clinical skincare store we worked on reached a 5.25% conversion rate in a $61,000 month after this kind of product page and store work.

Lever 4: tracking and signal quality

Ad platforms optimise toward the conversions they can see. If your pixel misses purchases, double-counts them or reports them late, the algorithm learns from bad data and spends money on the wrong people.

Set up the Conversions API alongside the pixel, deduplicate events with a shared event ID, send as much customer information as your privacy policy allows, and check event match quality in Events Manager. Clean signals do not show up as a line item, but they often lower CAC more than any single campaign change because every optimisation decision gets better.

  • Compare purchases in Events Manager with Shopify orders for the same days; a large gap means missing or duplicated events.
  • Make sure the purchase value includes the real order total, so value-based bidding has accurate data.
  • Fire add to cart and initiate checkout events too, so the platform has more signals while purchase volume is low.
  • Recheck tracking after every theme change, checkout app install or consent banner update.

Lever 5: audience consolidation

Many accounts split budget across dozens of small ad sets with narrow interests, lookalikes and overlapping audiences. Each one gets too little data to exit learning, and they bid against each other in the same auctions.

Consolidating into fewer campaigns with broad targeting gives the algorithm more conversions per ad set to learn from, which usually stabilises and lowers CAC. Keep separate structures only where there is a real reason, such as a different country, product line or margin. Use customer list exclusions so prospecting budget is not spent on people who already bought.

A simple structure works for most brands spending in the tens of thousands a month: one testing campaign where new creative gets a fair share of spend, one scaling campaign that receives the proven winners, and a small retargeting budget only if your prospecting campaigns do not already reach warm visitors. Fewer moving parts also make CAC easier to read, because you can see which change caused a shift instead of guessing across twenty ad sets.

Levers 6 and 7: retention, referral and channel mix

The cheapest customer is one you do not have to buy twice. Strong email and SMS flows, replenishment reminders and post-purchase cross-sells raise repeat purchase rate, which raises LTV and lets you accept a higher first-order CAC while blended CAC falls.

Referral

A simple give-and-get referral programme, sent after customers have had time to see results, brings in new customers at a known, fixed cost. Affiliate and creator programmes work the same way at larger scale.

Channel mix

Relying on one channel ties your CAC to its auction. Adding Google Shopping and search captures people already looking for your product, TikTok can open a younger or different audience, and organic content lowers blended CAC over time. Add channels one at a time and judge each on blended CAC and MER, not only platform-reported numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good CAC for an ecommerce brand?

A good CAC is one your margins and LTV can support. Compare CAC with contribution margin per first order and with 12-month LTV. If the first order covers CAC you can scale freely; if not, you need enough repeat purchases to pay it back within a period your cash flow can handle.

What is the fastest way to lower CAC?

Usually conversion rate and creative. Conversion rate improvements on high-traffic product pages lower CAC on every channel immediately. New creative angles can cut cost per purchase within a couple of weeks. Tracking fixes are also fast if your pixel or Conversions API setup is currently missing or duplicating purchases.

How do you calculate CAC?

Divide marketing spend by new customers acquired in the same period. For blended CAC use all marketing spend and all new customers. For paid CAC use ad spend and the customers those ads acquired. For paid traffic you can also think of it as cost per click divided by conversion rate.

Does raising AOV lower CAC?

Not directly. CAC is cost per customer, and AOV is revenue per order. But a higher AOV raises the contribution each first order brings in, so the same CAC becomes more profitable and you can afford to bid more. Bundles and quantity breaks can also lift conversion rate, which does lower CAC.

Why is my CAC going up?

Common causes are creative fatigue, seasonal auction costs such as Q4, tracking problems that hide conversions, too many small overlapping ad sets, a falling store conversion rate, or scaling budget faster than new creative arrives. Check frequency, CPC and site conversion rate first to see which part of the formula moved.
