Meta Andromeda Explained: What Changed for Ecommerce Ads

Direct Answer: Meta Andromeda is the AI retrieval system Meta uses to pick which ads, out of millions of candidates, could be shown to each person before the final ranking step in the auction. Meta described it on its engineering blog in December 2024. For ecommerce brands it means creative variety now decides reach: fewer, broader campaigns with many genuinely different ads beat narrow targeting and near duplicate creatives.

Meta Andromeda: Meta's next generation personalized ads retrieval engine, built on custom AI hardware, that narrows the full pool of eligible ads to a short list for each impression. Ranking models (including the GEM model) then choose the final ad and price. Andromeda powers Advantage+ automation and affects every advertiser; there is no opt in or opt out.

What is Meta Andromeda?

Every time someone opens Instagram or Facebook, Meta has a few milliseconds to choose one ad from tens of millions of eligible ads. It cannot score all of them with its heaviest models, so the job is split in two: a retrieval step that quickly finds a few thousand plausible candidates for that specific person, and a ranking step that scores those candidates precisely and runs the auction.

Andromeda is the new retrieval step. Meta announced it in the engineering post Meta Andromeda: Supercharging Advantage+ automation with the next-gen personalized ads retrieval engine on 2 December 2024. The key change is that retrieval itself became a large, personalised AI model running on Meta's own accelerator hardware, instead of a lighter heuristic filter. Meta reported higher ad quality and recall, and tied the system directly to Advantage+ campaign automation.

In plain words: the first cut used to be rough and mostly driven by your targeting settings. Now the first cut is a model that already understands the person well, and it picks the ads whose creative is most likely to interest that person. Your creative is doing the targeting.

How Meta Andromeda works (retrieval, then ranking)

Step 1: Retrieval narrows millions of ads to a short list per person. Andromeda looks at the person's signals and at embeddings of every eligible ad (what the creative shows and says, what people who engaged with it look like) and returns the candidates most likely to be relevant. If two of your ads are near duplicates, they land in the same place in that embedding space and effectively compete for one slot in the short list.

Step 2: Ranking models pick the final ad and the price. The retrieved candidates are scored by Meta's ranking models for predicted action rate, combined with the advertiser's bid and the ad quality signals, and the auction decides the winner and the price. This is where cost caps, bid caps and your optimisation event matter.

The practical consequence: an ad that never makes it through retrieval never gets the chance to prove itself in ranking, no matter how good the offer. Getting through retrieval depends on being different enough from your own other ads and relevant enough to a real slice of people.

Meta Andromeda vs GEM

People mix these up because Meta published both as parts of its ads AI stack. Andromeda is retrieval: it chooses which ads are candidates for a given person. GEM (Generative Ads Recommendation Model) is Meta's large ranking foundation model, described separately by Meta as a transformer based system trained across its ads data that improves how candidates are scored.

A simple way to hold it: Andromeda decides who gets to audition, GEM decides who gets the part. Both reward the same two things from an advertiser: creatives that are genuinely different from each other, and clean conversion signals so the models learn from real purchases rather than noise.

Meta Andromeda and creative diversity

The most visible change media buyers reported after the rollout: accounts with 12 ad sets each holding the same three creatives stopped scaling, while accounts with one or two campaigns and 20 clearly different ads kept growing. That is retrieval at work. Near duplicate ads (same video, new headline; same hook, new background music) are treated as one concept, so adding more of them does not add reach.

What different actually means. A new concept changes at least one of: the hook (the first 3 seconds and the claim), the angle (problem, mechanism, objection, identity, offer), the format (UGC testimonial, founder explainer, demo, split screen, static, carousel), or the person on camera. Changing the headline, the CTA button or the colour grade is an iteration, useful for squeezing a proven concept, but it does not open new reach.

The 45+ hook and angle matrix. For partner brands we brief creatives from a matrix of 5 angles by 3 hook styles by 3 formats. Every cell is a different concept to the retrieval model. A full matrix gives 45 distinct starting points; the weekly job is to fill the empty cells, kill the losers and iterate the winners. This is how accounts receive 30 to 50 new creatives a month without becoming 50 versions of one ad.

Meta Andromeda campaign structure

Consolidate. The structure that works under Andromeda is simpler than what most accounts still run: one Advantage+ sales campaign for the core catalogue with broad targeting, one testing campaign where new concepts launch, and optionally one retargeting campaign at 10% to 15% of spend using catalog ads. Interest stacks, lookalike ladders and geographic splits mostly fragment learning now. In our case library the pattern repeats: consolidating ad sets into simplified broad targeting Advantage+ campaigns is the step that unlocked scale.

When cost caps and bid caps still help. Cost controls do not fight Andromeda; they protect margin once a concept is proven. For a DTC partner we scaled $13,195 in spend into $54,552 in 7 days at 4.43x ROAS by duplicating the winning ad sets with bid caps set 20% higher than the original, which let the winners buy more volume without reopening the test budget.

Before and after. Before: 12 ad sets, 3 creatives each, interest targeting per ad set, 36 ads of which 30 were near duplicates. After: 2 campaigns, broad targeting, 20 distinct concepts plus 10 iterations of the top 3, a weekly refresh. Same spend, and the account exits learning faster because conversions are pooled.

Meta Andromeda best practices for ecommerce

1. Ship creative volume weekly. Aim for 8 to 12 new ads a week at $10K+ monthly spend, 30 to 50 a month at scale. Fatigue is now a reach problem, not just a frequency problem.

2. Split concepts from iterations. Each week, two thirds new concepts (new hook, angle, format or person), one third iterations of last week's winners.

3. Test inside one campaign. Launch new concepts in a single testing campaign with broad targeting so Meta compares them against each other on equal footing, then move winners into the scale campaign.

4. Match the landing page to the ad. The hook on the video must continue above the fold on the page; a dedicated landing page per angle lifts conversion rate and tells the ranking models the click was a good one.

5. Fix signal quality first. Conversions API with event match quality of 8 or higher, deduplicated with the pixel, so purchases feed the models rather than add to cart noise.

6. Give it patience windows. Judge a concept after 3 to 5 days or 2 to 3 times target CPA in spend, whichever comes first; do not edit live ad sets daily.

7. Read results on MER, not platform ROAS. Blended marketing efficiency ratio and new customer CAC catch the double counting that broad campaigns and retargeting produce in platform reports.

8. Refresh on a calendar. Put the Monday swipe file review and the Tuesday brief in the calendar; accounts that refresh by schedule rather than by panic have the flattest CPA curves.

What did not change

Andromeda changed delivery, not economics. The offer, the product, the landing page and your margins still decide whether scale is profitable. A 4x ROAS on a 40% margin product still loses money after returns and fees; a 2.5x ROAS on a 75% margin bundle can fund aggressive growth.

Is Meta Andromeda live? What it means in 2026

Yes. Meta stated in its December 2024 engineering post that Andromeda powers ads retrieval for Advantage+ automation, and performance patterns across our partner accounts changed in the way the post predicts: consolidated, creatively diverse accounts scale; fragmented, duplicate heavy accounts stall. Every advertiser is affected; there is no setting to turn it on or off.

What we can say with confidence as of 30 October 2026: creative diversity is the primary scaling lever, broad consolidated structures outperform segmented ones for most DTC brands, and cost controls remain useful for margin protection. What we avoid claiming: specific thresholds Meta has not published (an exact number of creatives, a precise similarity cut off). What is the best Meta Andromeda targeting for premium ecommerce brands?

Broad targeting with a wide range of genuinely different creatives. For premium products such as furniture, the creative does the targeting: show the product in real homes, at its real price point, with angles for different buyers, and let Andromeda match each ad to the people most likely to buy.

Interest targeting matters far less than before. Your creative now does most of the targeting, because different hooks, angles and formats are retrieved for different people.

Enough to test new concepts every week: roughly 8 to 12 new ads a week at $10K+ in monthly spend. Brand Scaling ships 30 to 50+ angle iterations a month for partner brands so accounts do not fatigue.

Andromeda is the retrieval stage that shortlists candidate ads for a person. GEM is Meta's large ranking model that scores those candidates. Both reward creative diversity and clean conversion signals.
