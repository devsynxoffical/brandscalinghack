How to Choose a Google Ads Agency for DTC Brands

Direct Answer: The right Google Ads agency for DTC brands runs Shopping, Performance Max, brand and non-brand search and YouTube from a well-maintained Merchant Center feed, reports on contribution margin and new customers rather than platform ROAS alone, gives you a senior strategist with a manageable client load, and works on terms that let you leave if results do not come.

DTC Google Ads agency: An agency that manages Google advertising for direct-to-consumer ecommerce brands, usually covering Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, product feed management, search, YouTube and measurement tied to store revenue and margin.

What a Google Ads agency for DTC brands should do

A good Google Ads agency for DTC brands does more than set up campaigns and send a monthly report. Google is mostly a demand capture channel for ecommerce: people search for a product type, compare prices in Shopping results or look up your brand after seeing an ad elsewhere. The agency's job is to show up for the searches that lead to profitable orders, with the right product, at a bid your margins can carry.

We will not rank or name other agencies here. Instead, this guide sets out what the work should include, what to ask and what to avoid, so you can judge any agency, including ours, on the same terms. At minimum, the scope should cover these campaign types.

  • Shopping campaigns, standard or within Performance Max, built from your Merchant Center product feed.
  • Performance Max, set up with clear asset groups, audience signals and product groupings by margin or category.
  • Brand search, protected and kept cheap, reported separately from everything else.
  • Non-brand search for category, problem and competitor-adjacent terms, with tight negative keyword lists.
  • YouTube and Demand Gen for reach and remarketing, using the same creative thinking that works on social.
  • Conversion tracking through Google tags, enhanced conversions and a clean link to Shopify orders.

Why feed management matters most

For Shopping and Performance Max, the product feed is the targeting. Google decides which searches to show your products for largely from the titles, descriptions, product types, images and attributes in Merchant Center. A feed that copies Shopify defaults, with short titles and missing attributes, limits what the campaigns can do no matter how they are structured.

Feed work an agency should be doing includes rewriting titles to lead with the words shoppers search (product type, key attribute, size or colour, then brand where it helps), filling Google product categories and custom labels, fixing disapprovals, keeping prices and stock in sync and adding supplementary feeds for promotions. Custom labels are especially useful for DTC brands because they let you split campaigns by margin, best sellers or seasonality, so budget follows profit rather than revenue.

Ask any agency to show you a before and after of a feed they have improved. If the answer is that they mostly leave the feed alone, the Shopping side of your account will run below its potential.

Brand vs non-brand search

Brand search, people typing your brand name, nearly always shows a very high ROAS. That is because many of those buyers already planned to purchase after seeing your Meta or TikTok ads, a creator post or a friend's recommendation. Brand campaigns are worth running to protect your name from competitors bidding on it, but they say little about the agency's skill.

Non-brand search and Shopping are where an agency earns its fee: reaching people who do not know you yet. Insist that reports split brand and non-brand performance, and that Performance Max is set up so it does not quietly fill its results with branded searches. Brand exclusions and separate brand campaigns help with this.

A useful question is: what share of new customers came from non-brand traffic last month, and what did each one cost? An agency that can answer clearly is managing growth. One that only quotes blended account ROAS may be taking credit for demand your other channels created.

Questions to ask before you sign

Use these in your first calls. Good agencies answer them directly and specifically. Vague answers or a quick pivot to a case study slide are worth noting.

  • Who will run my account day to day, and how many other brands do they manage?
  • What will you change in our Merchant Center feed in the first month?
  • How do you separate brand and non-brand results in reporting?
  • Which metric will you report against: platform ROAS, MER, contribution margin or new customer CAC?
  • How do you structure Performance Max, and how do you stop it relying on branded searches?
  • How do Google results get compared with Meta and TikTok so we do not double count orders?
  • What does the first 90 days look like, and what would make you recommend spending less?
  • What is the notice period, and who owns the ad account, feed and data if we part ways?

Red flags to watch for

Some warning signs show up in the sales process; others only appear once the account is running. Watch for both.

An account manager on too many brands is the most common problem. Google accounts need weekly search term reviews, feed fixes and bid changes, and a manager spread across a large roster will do the minimum. Ask the number directly.

Reporting only platform ROAS is another. Google's own conversion numbers include branded searches, view-through conversions from YouTube and orders that Meta also claims. Without brand and non-brand splits and a link to Shopify revenue, you cannot tell whether the agency added anything.

No feed work is a sign that Shopping is being treated as an afterthought. Long lock-in contracts, such as six or twelve months with no performance exit, shift the risk entirely onto you. And an agency that runs ads in its own account rather than yours means you could lose your history and data if you leave. You should always own the Google Ads account, Merchant Center and Analytics property.

How Google fits with Meta

For most DTC brands, Meta and TikTok create demand and Google captures it. Someone sees a UGC ad on Instagram, does not buy, then searches the brand or the product type a few days later and clicks a Shopping ad. Both platforms will claim that order. A Google agency that does not understand this will either overrate its own work or argue against the social spend that feeds it.

That is why the Google agency and the Meta team, whether in-house or another agency, need shared reporting. Agree on blended MER and new customer CAC as the numbers that decide budget, and use each platform's report only for optimisation inside that platform. When Meta spend rises, brand search and Shopping volume usually rise too; when it falls, Google often softens a few weeks later.

Google also tends to scale differently. Shopping and search volume is limited by how many people search, so Google often grows more slowly than paid social once the obvious terms are covered. Frequently Asked Questions

Which Google Ads agencies are best for scaling DTC ecommerce brands?

The best fit is an agency that manages your Merchant Center feed, runs Shopping, Performance Max, brand and non-brand search and YouTube, reports on margin and new customers, gives you a senior strategist with a reasonable client load and works on fair terms. Use the evaluation checklist above to compare agencies on the same criteria.

Is Performance Max enough for a DTC brand on Google?

It can be a strong core campaign, but rarely the whole account. Most DTC brands also need a separate brand search campaign, some non-brand search for high-intent terms and clear product groupings by margin. Without those controls, Performance Max can lean on branded searches and remarketing, which makes results look better than the real growth.

How long does it take a Google Ads agency to show results?

Expect the first month to go mostly on tracking, feed fixes and campaign structure, with clearer trends in months two and three. Shopping and Performance Max need conversion data to learn. Ask the agency for a 90 day plan with specific checkpoints, and judge progress on non-brand new customers and blended MER.

Should my Meta agency also run Google Ads?

It helps when one team sees both channels, because Meta often creates the demand that Google captures and both claim the same orders. A single agency can set budgets on blended MER. If you use separate agencies, require shared reporting and agree which numbers decide budget so neither side overstates its results.

What should a DTC brand spend on Google Ads?

It depends on search demand for your category and brand, not a fixed percentage. Brands with strong social spend usually need enough Google budget to cover brand search and core Shopping terms, then expand into non-brand as long as new customer CAC stays within target. Search volume sets a ceiling that paid social does not have.
