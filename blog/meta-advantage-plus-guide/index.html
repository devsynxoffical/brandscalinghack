Advantage Plus Shopping Campaigns: A Practical Guide for DTC Brands

Direct Answer: Advantage plus shopping campaigns are Meta's automated sales campaigns for ecommerce. You supply budget, a conversion goal, a catalog and a set of creatives, and Meta decides who sees which ad across Facebook and Instagram. They suit accounts with clean purchase tracking and varied creative, with manual campaigns kept for tight control and testing.

Advantage+ shopping (sales) campaign: A Meta campaign type that automates targeting, placements and much of the budget allocation for purchase-focused ecommerce campaigns. Meta has renamed and merged these options over time, so the label in your Ads Manager may differ.

What are Advantage+ shopping campaigns?

Advantage plus shopping campaigns, often shortened to ASC, are Meta's automated campaign type for ecommerce sales. Instead of building ad sets with interests, lookalikes and placement choices, you give Meta a budget, a purchase goal, your country and your creative. Meta's systems then decide who to show ads to, on which placements, and which creative to serve to each person.

Meta has changed the names and setup of these campaigns more than once. In many accounts the option now appears as an Advantage+ sales campaign, and some controls have moved or merged. The principles in this guide hold across versions: automation works best when you give it clean signals and good creative, and you keep control through budget, creative and a few settings rather than through targeting.

Advantage+ depends on how Meta finds people for each ad. Our guide to Meta Andromeda explains why creative variety, not targeting, now decides how far these campaigns can reach.

How to set up an Advantage+ campaign

Get the foundations right before launching. Advantage+ amplifies whatever signal you give it, including bad data.

  • Confirm tracking: pixel and Conversions API both sending Purchase events, deduplicated, with good event match quality.
  • Connect your product catalog if you plan to use catalog or dynamic ads.
  • Define existing customers by uploading or syncing customer lists, and add your website and engagement audiences where the setting allows.
  • Choose the sales objective and the Advantage+ option, with purchase as the conversion event.
  • Set one daily budget large enough to produce regular purchases for learning.
  • Add 5 to 10 genuinely different creatives at launch: video, static and catalog formats.
  • Leave it alone for the first several days while it learns, unless spend is clearly wasted.

The existing customer budget cap

Left alone, an automated campaign often finds the easiest conversions: people who already know and buy from you. That inflates ROAS while bringing in few new customers.

To manage this, Meta has offered a way to tell it who your existing customers are and to limit the share of budget spent on them, often called the existing customer budget cap. Depending on your account and the current version, it may appear as a percentage cap, as customer segment settings, or as reporting that splits results by new and existing customers. Set it up as soon as it is available in your account.

A practical starting point for a prospecting-focused campaign is a low cap on existing customers, then adjusting based on blended results. If email and SMS already handle repeat purchases well, there is less reason to pay Meta for them.

Creative inputs: what to feed it

With targeting automated, creative becomes the main lever you control. Each distinct ad helps Meta find a different slice of buyers, while near-duplicates compete for the same people.

Feed the campaign variety: different angles (problem, result, objection, offer), different hook styles and different formats (UGC video, demo, static, carousel, catalog). Add new creative regularly so the campaign does not lean on one ad until it fatigues. For partner brands we produce 30 to 50+ new creatives a month, and the best performers from testing graduate into Advantage+.

Check the creative enhancement settings. Meta can apply automatic edits such as cropping, music or text variations. Some help, some change how your brand looks, so review them rather than accepting every default.

Formats and placements

Supply vertical 9:16 video for Reels and Stories and a 4:5 or 1:1 version for the feed, so Meta does not have to crop your ads awkwardly. Keep key text and the product away from the edges where interface elements sit. If you sell a wide catalog, add a catalog or collection ad alongside your videos so the campaign can show people products similar to what they viewed.

Copy and landing pages

Write several primary texts and headlines that match the angles in your videos, rather than one generic line for every ad. Send each ad to the page that fits its promise: a bundle ad to the bundle page, a single-product demo to that product page. Automation can find the right person, but it cannot fix a landing page that does not deliver what the ad promised.

When to use manual campaigns instead

Advantage+ is not always the answer. Keep manual campaigns for jobs where control matters more than automation. The two are not rivals: in a healthy account manual campaigns find and prove ads, and Advantage+ scales them. If you notice that every result depends on one automated campaign and you cannot explain why it works, that is a sign to rebuild some manual structure so you can learn again.

  • Creative testing: a manual testing campaign gives each new ad a fair, readable share of spend before it graduates.
  • Low purchase volume: with few conversions a week, automation has too little data to learn from.
  • Specific audiences: launches to a waitlist, regional offers or a product meant for a narrow group.
  • Strict cost control: bid caps and cost caps on separate campaigns during peaks such as BFCM.
  • New markets: when you want clean, separate reads on a new country.

Reporting on Advantage+ campaigns

Advantage+ reporting shows fewer levers, so read it carefully. Look at results by creative to see which ads are carrying spend, and use the breakdowns for new versus existing customers where they are available. A high ROAS driven mostly by existing customers is not growth.

Judge the campaign against your blended numbers, not only Ads Manager. Compare new customer revenue, blended CAC and MER before and after launch. Platform-reported ROAS includes conversions that would have happened anyway, and automated campaigns can be especially good at claiming them. If blended results hold or improve as Advantage+ spend grows, it is working.

  • Weekly: spend and cost per purchase by creative, frequency, new versus existing customer split.
  • Monthly: blended CAC, MER and new customer revenue against the previous month.
  • Before scaling: confirm efficiency held for at least a week, then raise budget in 20 to 30% steps.
  • Every time: compare purchase counts in Ads Manager with Shopify orders to catch tracking drift.

Common Advantage+ mistakes

Most underperforming Advantage+ campaigns fail for the same few reasons. Almost all of them come back to the same idea: the campaign can only optimise with the signals, creative and time you give it. When results drop, check those three inputs before you blame the algorithm or rebuild the account.

  • Launching with weak or missing Conversions API data.
  • Not defining existing customers, so budget goes to repeat buyers.
  • Too few creatives, or several near-identical versions of one ad.
  • Editing budgets and creative daily, which keeps the campaign relearning.
  • Running several Advantage+ campaigns that compete for the same buyers.
  • Judging success on platform ROAS alone instead of blended CAC and MER.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Advantage+ and a regular campaign?

In Advantage+ shopping or sales campaigns, Meta automates targeting, placements and much of the budget allocation. In a regular manual campaign you build the ad sets, choose audiences and set exclusions. Advantage+ is usually better for scaling proven creative, while manual campaigns suit testing and situations that need tighter control.

How much budget does an Advantage+ campaign need?

Enough to generate regular purchases so the system can learn, which depends on your cost per purchase. A common approach is a daily budget that can fund several purchases per day at your target CPA. Run your numbers in a budget calculator and start lower if purchase volume is limited.

Should I exclude existing customers from Advantage+?

Usually you should limit them rather than fully exclude them. Define existing customers with your customer lists, then cap the share of budget spent on them using the setting available in your account. If your email and SMS flows already drive repeat orders, keep that cap low.

How many creatives should I add to Advantage+?

Start with about 5 to 10 genuinely different creatives covering several angles and formats, then add new ones regularly. Different concepts help Meta reach different buyers, while near-identical versions mostly compete for the same people. Move proven winners in from your testing campaign rather than launching untested ads.

Can I run Advantage+ and manual campaigns together?

Yes, and most ecommerce accounts should. A common structure uses a manual campaign for creative testing, an Advantage+ campaign for scaling winners and, at peak times, cost-controlled campaigns for extra volume. Keep the number of campaigns small so they do not compete against each other in the same auctions.

Why did my Advantage+ campaign stop performing?

The usual causes are creative fatigue, too few new ads, rising seasonal costs, budget changes that reset learning, or tracking problems. Check frequency and results by creative first, then confirm Purchase events are still reporting correctly. Adding fresh, distinct creative fixes most slowdowns.
