Shopify CRO Tips: A 10-Point Conversion Checklist

Direct Answer: The Shopify CRO tips that move revenue most are fast mobile load times, a clear offer above the fold, product pages that answer objections, visible reviews, bundles that lift order value, a cart drawer with upsells, a short checkout, post-purchase offers and a habit of testing one change at a time.

Conversion rate optimization (CRO): The practice of increasing the share of visitors who buy, and how much they spend, by improving your store's speed, pages, offers and checkout based on data rather than opinion.

The 10-point Shopify CRO checklist at a glance

These Shopify CRO tips are ordered the way a shopper moves through your store, from the first second of loading to the thank-you page. Work through them in order, because a fix early in the journey affects every step after it. If your traffic comes mainly from paid social, most of it lands on mobile product pages, so start there.

Before changing anything, record your baseline for a few weeks: conversion rate by device and traffic source, add to cart rate, checkout completion rate, AOV and revenue per session. Without a baseline you cannot tell whether a change helped, and you risk keeping changes that only looked good during a strong sales week.

Speed: the fix that helps every page

A slow store loses shoppers before they see the product, and paid traffic is the least patient traffic you have. Test your key pages in PageSpeed Insights on mobile, and look at real-user data where it exists rather than desktop scores.

  • Remove apps you no longer use, because many leave scripts behind even after you stop using them.
  • Compress images, serve modern formats and avoid giant hero videos above the fold.
  • Load reviews, chat widgets and tracking scripts after the main content where possible.
  • Use a lightweight, well-maintained theme rather than stacking customisations on an old one.
  • Limit custom fonts to one or two weights.

Check speed after every app install

Speed decays slowly as apps, sections and scripts pile up. Re-test your main product page whenever you add an app or a new section, and keep a short list of every script on the store and why it is there. Anything nobody can explain is a candidate for removal.

Above the fold and the product page

On a phone, the first screen decides whether someone scrolls or leaves. Show the product clearly, a one-line benefit, the price, the star rating and the add to cart button without any scrolling. Match the headline to the ad that sent the visitor, so the promise in the ad continues on the page.

Below the fold, the product page has one job: answer every objection before the shopper has to ask. List the questions customers send your support team and answer them on the page.

  • Who it is for and the result they can expect, in plain language.
  • How to use it, with a short video or image sequence.
  • Ingredients, materials, sizing or specifications, with a size guide where relevant.
  • Shipping times, returns and guarantee close to the add to cart button.
  • A comparison with the alternatives the shopper is weighing up.
  • An FAQ section built from real customer questions.

Reviews, offers and bundles

Reviews do the selling a salesperson would do in a shop. Put the star rating and review count next to the product title, then show photo and video reviews further down, ideally filterable by concern, size or skin type. Ask for reviews in your post-purchase emails so the count keeps growing.

Offers and bundles raise order value, which means each visitor is worth more even if conversion rate stays the same. Quantity breaks such as buy two, save more suit consumables. Curated bundles suit routines and sets. A free gift above a spend threshold works well when the gift costs little but feels valuable. Show the saving clearly in money, not just as a percentage, and make the bundle the default choice where it suits most buyers.

Check margin before you launch any offer. A bundle that lifts AOV but gives away most of the profit on each order can raise revenue while lowering contribution margin. Work out the profit on each bundle option at its discounted price, and design the ladder so the option you want most buyers to choose is also the one that earns you the most.

Cart drawer and checkout

A cart drawer that slides open on add to cart keeps shoppers on the page and moving toward checkout. Use it well.

  • Show a progress bar toward free shipping, set just above your current AOV.
  • Offer one relevant add-on, such as a refill or accessory, not a wall of products.
  • Repeat the guarantee and delivery estimate.
  • Make the checkout button the most obvious element in the drawer.

Checkout

Shopify's checkout is already well optimised, so focus on what you control. Turn on express payment options such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay. Show shipping costs before checkout so there are no surprises. Keep discount code fields from sending shoppers off to hunt for codes, for example by applying offers automatically.

Post-purchase and mobile

The moment after purchase is the easiest time to sell more, because the customer has just decided to trust you. A one-click post-purchase upsell, shown before the thank-you page, adds revenue without touching conversion rate. Offer something that complements what they bought, at a modest discount, with no need to re-enter payment details. Then use the confirmation page and emails to set delivery expectations and reduce buyer's remorse.

Most paid social traffic is mobile, so review every step above on a real phone, not a desktop preview. Make buttons large enough for a thumb, add a sticky add to cart bar that appears on scroll, keep menus short, and check that pop-ups do not cover the product on small screens. Put a timer on your own checkout run: if it feels slow to you, it feels slower to a stranger.

Testing changes the right way

Change one thing at a time and measure it on real orders, not clicks. If traffic is high enough, run a proper A/B test. If it is not, make one change, keep traffic sources steady and compare a few weeks before and after, knowing the result is directional.

Small lifts are worth chasing. Example arithmetic only: a store with 20,000 monthly sessions and a $70 AOV.

What this looks like on a real store

When we rebuilt a clinical skincare store around this checklist, focusing on speed, product page structure, reviews and bundles, it reached a 5.25% store conversion rate and a $61,000 month. The work was not a redesign for its own sake. Each change answered a specific question: why do visitors leave, what do they need to see, and what would make them buy more than one.

Run through the ten points on your own store this week. Most brands find two or three gaps they can close quickly, and those quick fixes often matter more than any new campaign.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good conversion rate for a Shopify store?

It varies with traffic source, price point and product, so compare against your own history rather than a general number. Paid social traffic usually converts lower than email or returning visitors. Track conversion rate by source and device, then judge each change by whether it lifts revenue per session.

Which Shopify CRO tip should I start with?

Start with speed and the mobile product page, because most paid traffic lands there and every later step depends on it. Make sure the product, benefit, price, reviews and add to cart button are visible on the first screen of a phone, then work down the checklist in order.

Do bundles really increase Shopify revenue?

They often raise average order value, which makes each visitor worth more even if conversion rate stays flat. Quantity breaks suit consumables and curated sets suit routines. Show the saving in money, make the bundle easy to choose and check that margin still works after the discount.

Are post-purchase upsells worth adding?

Usually, yes. A one-click offer after payment adds revenue without risking the main conversion, because the customer has already bought. Keep it relevant to the first purchase, price it sensibly and avoid asking for payment details again. Measure take rate and any change in refunds.

How do I A/B test on Shopify with low traffic?

With low traffic, a formal A/B test can take too long to reach a reliable answer. Make one change at a time, hold traffic sources steady and compare several weeks before and after. Prioritise changes with a clear reason behind them, such as answering a frequent customer question.
