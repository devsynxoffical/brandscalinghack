How to Find Winning Products: The Research and Testing Process Behind Real Stores

Direct Answer: A winning product solves a clear problem, can be shown working in a short video, leaves enough margin to pay for ads, and is not easy to buy cheaper elsewhere. Find candidates by studying long-running ads in the Facebook Ad Library, organic TikTok content and customer reviews, then prove them with small ad tests judged against your break-even cost per purchase. Expect most products to fail: in published cases, stores tested around 23 to 25 products to find their winners.

Winning product: A product that sells profitably through paid ads at a cost per purchase below your break-even point, and keeps doing so as the budget increases.

What makes a winning product

A winning product is not just a product that sells. It sells profitably through ads and keeps selling as the budget grows. The products behind the results on this site tend to share the same traits.

  • It solves a real problem or creates an obvious before and after.
  • It can be demonstrated in a few seconds of video, so the ad sells it.
  • Its margin leaves room for ad costs: check break-even ROAS before testing.
  • It is not a commodity buyers can get cheaper and faster on a marketplace.
  • It ships reliably, with low breakage and refund risk.
  • It has room for bundles or upsells, which raise order value.

Where to find product ideas

The best product ideas come from evidence that real people are already buying. These are the sources we use.

Facebook Ad Library

Search a product type and look for ads that have been running for two weeks or more. Advertisers rarely keep paying for ads that lose money, so long-running ads are a strong signal.

Organic TikTok and Instagram

Videos where people show a product solving a problem, with comments asking where to buy it, show demand before ads saturate it.

Customer reviews

Reviews on large marketplaces show what buyers love and what they complain about. Complaints about a popular product point to a better version you could sell.

Competitor stores

Stores that keep a product at the top of their catalog, run bundles on it and advertise it consistently are usually making money on it.

Why spy tools alone are not enough

Spy tools and product apps show the same trending products to thousands of stores at the same time. By the time a product appears at the top of those lists, many competitors are already advertising it, costs rise and margins fall.

Use tools to find ideas, then do the research yourself: check how long ads have run, read reviews, compare prices and calculate your margin. The $148K-in-a-day case makes the same point: the product research behind it was done manually rather than taken from spy tools and apps.

How to test products with ads

Research only produces candidates. Ads prove which ones are winners. Before testing, calculate your break-even cost per purchase: your price multiplied by the share of it left after product cost, shipping, fees and refunds.

Launch each product with several creatives on different angles, broad targeting and purchase as the optimisation event. Judge it on cost per purchase, not on clicks or likes. A product that sells below break-even cost per purchase for several days is a candidate winner; one that spends about twice your break-even cost per purchase without a sale is usually cut.

What to do once you find a winner

Finding a winner is the start, not the finish. The stores that went on to large results did three things next.

They built the store around the product: the $148K-in-a-day store was niched around its winner before it scaled. They kept the creative fresh, adding new hooks on the winning angle as frequency rose. And they raised order value with bundles and upsells, which lets the ads afford a higher cost per customer as the budget grows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a winning product in dropshipping?

A product that sells profitably through paid ads, at a cost per purchase below your break-even point, and keeps doing so as the budget increases. Selling a few units is not enough; it has to stay profitable at scale.

How do I find winning products for free?

Use the free Facebook Ad Library to find ads that have run for two weeks or more, browse organic TikTok and Instagram for products people ask about, and read marketplace reviews. Then check the margin before spending anything on tests.

How many products should I test before finding a winner?

Plan for many. In published cases on this site, one store tested 25 products to find one consistent winner, and another tested about 23 to find 7.

Are winning product lists worth buying?

Usually not on their own. Lists and spy tools show the same products to many stores at once, which raises competition. Use them for ideas, then do your own research and testing.

What margin does a winning product need?

Enough that your break-even ROAS is realistic. If 40% of the price is left after product cost, shipping, fees and refunds, you break even at 2.5x ROAS; at 25% you need 4x, which is much harder to sustain.
