Black Friday Discount Strategy: How Deep Can a DTC Brand Afford to Go?

Direct Answer: A Black Friday discount strategy should start from contribution margin, not from what competitors are offering. Work out profit per order before and after the discount, then how many extra orders and what ROAS you need to stay ahead. Often a moderate discount, a bundle or a gift with purchase earns more than a deep percentage off.

Black Friday discount strategy: The plan for which offer a brand runs over Black Friday and Cyber Monday, how deep it goes, who sees it first and when it ends, set so that the extra orders more than replace the margin the discount gives away.

Why your Black Friday discount strategy starts with margin

Most founders pick a Black Friday discount strategy by looking at what everyone else is doing. A competitor runs 30 percent off, so 30 percent feels like the minimum, and 40 percent feels like the way to win. The problem is that a discount does not come out of revenue in a neat proportion. It comes straight out of the money left after product, shipping and fees, which is a much smaller number. A 25 percent discount can remove close to half of the profit on an order.

That is why the right question is not "what discount will people respond to?" but "how much margin do I have, and how many extra orders do I need to replace what the discount removes?" Once you can answer that for your own products, the choice between 15, 25 and 40 percent stops being a guess.

The contribution margin math behind a discount

Contribution margin per order is what is left from an order after the costs that only exist because the order happened: the product, getting it to the customer and the payment fee. Ad cost is left out here on purpose, because the point is to find out how much you can afford to pay for each order. Five formulas cover everything you need.

  • Margin before discount = price minus COGS minus shipping minus (price times the payment fee rate).
  • Discounted price = price times (1 minus the discount rate).
  • Margin after discount = discounted price minus COGS minus shipping minus (discounted price times the payment fee rate).
  • Orders needed for the same profit = margin before discount divided by margin after discount.
  • Break-even ROAS after discount = discounted price divided by margin after discount.

Why the fee moves but COGS does not

Payment processing fees are a percentage of what the customer pays, so they shrink a little when the price drops. COGS and shipping do not. You still buy the same product and send the same parcel whether the customer paid full price or 40 percent less. That fixed chunk of cost is why margin falls much faster than price as the discount gets deeper.

Worked example: 0 to 40 percent off an $80 order

This is an example, not a benchmark. It uses an $80 order with $24 COGS, $9 shipping and fulfilment and a 3 percent payment fee. At full price, the fee is $2.40, so the contribution margin is $44.60 per order. Here is what happens at three common discount levels.

  • At 15 percent off, the price falls by 15 percent but margin per order falls by about 26 percent, so you need 1.35 orders for every one you would have sold at full price.
  • At 25 percent off, you need 1.77 times the orders to make the same contribution profit, before any ad cost.
  • At 40 percent off, you need 3.29 times the orders, and the ROAS needed just to break even rises from 1.79x to 3.54x.

How deep a discount you can actually afford

Read the last column of the table as a demand test. A 40 percent discount only pays off if the offer more than triples order volume compared with what the same traffic would buy at full price. Some brands do see that kind of lift on hero products with strong pent-up demand, but most should not plan around it. A 15 to 25 percent offer needs a far smaller lift to work, which is a much safer bet when ad costs are also rising.

Run the numbers per product, not per store. A product with high COGS and heavy shipping can lose money at a discount that is comfortable on a lighter, higher-margin product. Many brands end up with a mix: a strong offer on the products where margin can carry it and a smaller offer, or a bundle, on the rest.

Also count the orders you would have had anyway. Customers who were going to buy in late November at full price will happily take the discount, and that lost margin is real even though no report shows it. The deeper the offer, the more of those orders you pay away. Alternatives to deep percentage discounts

A strong Black Friday offer does not have to be a big number off everything. These options often feel just as generous to the customer while protecting more margin, because they raise order value or use costs you control.

Bundles

Bundle two or three products that are often bought together at a price below buying them separately. The customer gets a clear saving, while your shipping and fee cost is spread across a bigger order, so margin per order often ends up higher than a straight percentage off a single item.

Gift with purchase

A free item added to the order costs you its COGS, not its retail price. A sample size, accessory or slow-moving product can carry a high perceived value at a low real cost. Make the gift visible in the creative and on the product page, not just at checkout.

Tiered spend thresholds

Offers such as a smaller saving above one spend level and a bigger saving above a higher one push customers to add items. Set the tiers just above your current average order value so the discount is earned by a bigger basket.

Free shipping thresholds

If shipping is a large cost line, a free shipping threshold set above your normal order value both removes a common checkout objection and lifts basket size. It can stack with a modest percentage offer.

Early access for subscribers

Give email and SMS subscribers the offer a day or two before it goes public. It rewards the list, grows sign-ups in the weeks before and moves orders to your cheapest channel. One of our published results, $112,866 in 13 days on $28,391 of spend, came from a brand that launched early BFCM offers.

How the discount changes CPA and ROAS targets on Meta

Your contribution margin per order is the most you can pay to acquire that order and break even. In the example, that is $44.60 at full price but only $25.20 at 25 percent off. If your normal cost per purchase on Meta is $30, it is comfortably profitable at full price and loses money during the sale unless the offer lowers it.

That can happen, because a good offer usually lifts conversion rate. In this example, getting a $30 CPA down to $25.20 at the same cost per click needs a conversion rate about 19 percent higher just to break even. Ad costs also tend to rise in late November as more brands compete for the same placements, so that lift has to cover both the thinner margin and pricier traffic.

So before the sale starts, reset every target in the account to the discounted numbers. Update cost caps and bid caps to the new break-even CPA minus the profit you want per order. Change the ROAS you judge campaigns against, because 2.0x might be profitable at full price in this example and unprofitable at 25 percent off. Send the offer to email and SMS first

The cheapest Black Friday orders come from people who already know you. Email and SMS reach them without paying for the impression, which matters most in the one week when paid traffic is most expensive. Plan the owned channel sequence before you plan the ads.

A typical sequence starts with a teaser and a sign-up push in the weeks before, then early access for subscribers, the public launch, reminders over the weekend, a Cyber Monday message and a clear last call. Segment it: past buyers can get cross-sell bundles rather than the acquisition offer on what they already own, and engaged non-buyers get the main offer with the strongest proof.

Run paid ads mostly to new customers and to warm visitors who never gave you an email or phone number. Exclude recent purchasers from discount ads where you can, so you are not paying to show a discount to people who would open the email anyway.

What to do after Cyber Monday

End the offer when you said it would end. Extending a sale again and again teaches customers that the deadline is not real, which weakens every future promotion. If you want a softer landing, move to a smaller offer such as a gift with purchase for the shipping cutoff period rather than keeping the full discount live.

Then reset the ad account. Return cost caps, bid caps and ROAS targets to full-price margins, swap discount creative for evergreen and gifting creative, and expect conversion rate to drop for a few days as shoppers pause. The new customers you acquired are the real return on the sale, so move them into a post-purchase flow with product education, a review request and a timed second-purchase offer that is smaller than the Black Friday one.

Finally, write down the real numbers while they are fresh: orders, AOV, contribution margin after the discount, ad spend and how many buyers were new. Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a DTC brand discount for Black Friday?

Work it out from your contribution margin. Calculate profit per order at full price and at each discount you are considering, then how many extra orders each one needs to earn the same profit. In our $80 example, 25 percent off needs 1.77 times the orders and 40 percent off needs 3.29 times. Choose the deepest offer your realistic demand can carry.

Is 40 percent off ever worth it for Black Friday?

It can be when margins are very high or when the goal is clearing stock or acquiring customers with strong repeat purchase rates. For most products with meaningful COGS and shipping, a 40 percent discount needs several times the normal order volume just to match full-price profit, and the break-even ROAS rises sharply. Test the math first.

How do I calculate break-even ROAS after a discount?

Divide the discounted price by the contribution margin after the discount. Contribution margin is the discounted price minus COGS, shipping and the payment fee on the discounted price. In the example in this guide, an $80 order at 25 percent off sells for $60, leaves $25.20 of margin and needs a 2.38x ROAS to break even.

What are good Black Friday offers besides a percentage discount?

Bundles, a gift with purchase, tiered savings based on spend, a free shipping threshold above your average order value and early access for email and SMS subscribers. These often feel generous to customers while protecting more margin, because they raise order value or cost you the product cost of a gift rather than a cut in price.

Should I change my Meta cost caps during Black Friday?

Yes. A discount lowers the most you can pay for an order, so cost caps, bid caps and ROAS targets set for full-price margins will let campaigns overspend during the sale. Reset them to the discounted break-even numbers before launch, then set them back to full-price targets as soon as the offer ends after Cyber Monday.

When should a Black Friday sale end?

On the date you announced, usually the end of Cyber Monday, which is November 30 in 2026. Repeated extensions train customers to ignore deadlines. If you want to keep some momentum into December, switch to a smaller offer such as a gift with purchase and focus messaging on gifting and shipping cutoffs.
