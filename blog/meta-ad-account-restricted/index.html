Meta Ad Account Restricted or Disabled: What It Means and How to Get It Back

Direct Answer: A restricted or disabled Meta ad account means Meta has stopped that account, your profile or your whole business portfolio from running ads, usually because its systems flagged a policy issue, a payment problem or suspicious activity. Check the status in Account Quality, read the reason Meta gives, fix what you can, and request a review there. Do not open new accounts to keep advertising, because Meta treats that as circumvention.

Meta ad account restriction: A limit Meta places on an ad account, a person's ability to advertise, or a business portfolio when it believes Meta's Advertising Standards, Community Standards or payment terms have been broken. Restricted assets cannot run ads until the issue is resolved or a review succeeds.

Disabled, restricted, portfolio restricted or ad rejected?

There are four different problems that people describe as "my ad account got banned", and each one is fixed differently. Work out which one you have before you do anything else.

  • An ad rejection is the mildest. You can edit the ad or request a review of that ad on its own.
  • Repeated rejections matter, though. A pattern of rejected ads can lead to wider restrictions on the account or the people running it.
  • A profile restriction follows the person, not the brand. If your media buyer's profile is restricted, adding a different admin may let the brand keep advertising while that person appeals.

Where to check your status

Check your status in Account Quality, Meta's page for the restrictions on your profile, ad accounts, Pages and business portfolio. Meta's current help pages use two names for it: some send you to Account Quality, and the ads review policy page sends you to Business Support Home. Use whichever your account shows; both list the restriction, its reason and the request review option.

You can usually reach Account Quality from Meta Business Suite, from the menu in Ads Manager, or from the notice Meta shows when an asset is restricted. It lists each restricted asset, the reason Meta gives, and whether you can request a review. Meta also sends notices by email and in the Business Suite notifications, so check those too.

Read the stated reason closely. "Unacceptable business practices", "circumventing systems", "unpaid balance" and a specific ad policy all point to different fixes. If the reason is unclear, open the Meta Business Help Center for the latest guidance on that reason, because Meta updates the wording, menus and options regularly.

How to request a review

If you believe the restriction is a mistake, or you have fixed the cause, request a review from Account Quality. Select the restricted asset, choose the option to request a review, and follow the steps Meta shows. The steps vary by restriction type and region, and Meta may ask you to confirm your identity, for example with an ID document or a short video selfie, or to secure your account first.

Before you submit, do the groundwork:

  • Fix what caused it. Edit or remove ads that break policy, clean up landing pages and settle any unpaid balance.
  • Secure every profile involved. Turn on two-factor authentication and remove any admins or partners you do not recognise.
  • Keep your explanation short and factual if Meta gives you a space to write one. Explain what the business sells and what you changed.
  • Request one review per asset and wait. The number of reviews you can request may be limited, and some decisions are final.
  • Keep a record of dates, screenshots and the reasons shown, in case you need to contact Meta support or your rep later.

How long does a review take?

Meta does not commit to a fixed review time, and it varies by case. Some reviews come back quickly and others take much longer. Account Quality shows when a review is in progress and the outcome when it is done. If you have a Meta marketing rep through an agency or your own spend, they may be able to help you find the right support route, but they cannot overrule a policy decision.

Common causes for DTC brands

Most restrictions on DTC brands come from ad copy and claims, payment problems, unusual account behaviour or a poor buyer experience. Meta's systems are largely automated, so it is the pattern of signals that matters, not intent. These are the causes we see most often.

  • Health and before-and-after claims. Supplements, skincare and weight management ads that promise outcomes or show dramatic before-and-after images often break Meta's policies on health and personal health claims.
  • Personal attributes wording. Meta does not allow ads that assert or imply someone's personal attributes, such as a health condition or financial situation. "Struggling with acne?" or "Tired of your belly fat?" can be enough.
  • Payment failures. A declined card or an unpaid balance can disable an ad account until the balance is settled.
  • Sudden spend spikes on new accounts. A brand-new ad account that jumps straight to a large daily budget can look like a compromised or fraudulent account.
  • Circumvention signals. Links to previously disabled accounts or profiles, landing pages that show reviewers something different from users, or using someone else's account to keep advertising.
  • Low customer feedback. Meta collects feedback from people who bought after seeing your ads. A low feedback score, often from slow shipping or products that do not match the ad, can reduce delivery and in some cases lead to restrictions.
  • Compromised accounts. A hacked admin profile that runs unfamiliar ads, often in another currency or market, can get the whole portfolio restricted.

Higher-risk categories

Supplements, health and wellness, skincare, weight management and anything that touches medical claims draw the most scrutiny. If you sell in these categories, write ads that describe the product and the experience of using it rather than promising a result, and make sure the landing page follows the same rules. Our health and wellness and supplement pages cover how we approach compliant creative for these brands.

Payment failures and unpaid balances

A payment restriction is usually the quickest to fix: pay the outstanding balance and the account can often return to normal. Go to Billing and payments for the ad account, check the failed charge, and either retry it or add a working payment method and pay the balance.

To stop it happening again, keep a backup payment method on the account, make sure your card limit can cover your daily spend, and tell your bank to expect regular charges from Meta. Give each ad account its own payment method where you can, so one failed card does not affect every account in the portfolio. If the account stays disabled after the balance is paid, request a review from Account Quality.

What not to do

Do not open new ad accounts, profiles or business portfolios to keep advertising while you are restricted. Meta treats this as circumvention, and it is one of the surest ways to turn a fixable restriction into a permanent one across every asset you own.

Avoid these as well:

  • Buying, renting or borrowing ad accounts or profiles from other people.
  • Paying "unban" services that ask for your login details. They often cannot do what they promise, and sharing logins puts your assets at further risk.
  • Resubmitting the same rejected ad again and again without changing it.
  • Using landing pages or redirects that show Meta's reviewers something different from what customers see.
  • Deleting the assets or admins involved and starting over. Meta can link new assets to old ones.

Prevention checklist

The best defence against a restricted Meta ad account is a well-set-up business portfolio, a gradual spend ramp and ads and pages that follow policy from the start. Work through this list once, then review it whenever you add a new ad account or team member. A Meta ads audit should include the same checks.

  • Verify your business in the business portfolio settings, using your registered business details.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for every person with access, and require it in the portfolio settings.
  • Have at least two trusted admins, so one restricted or locked-out profile does not lock the brand out.
  • Give agencies and freelancers partner access through their own business portfolio rather than sharing logins.
  • Ramp spend gradually on new ad accounts instead of starting at full budget.
  • Keep landing pages compliant: clear business details, contact information, shipping and returns policies, and claims that match the ads.
  • Use separate payment methods for each ad account, plus a backup card.
  • Check your ads against Meta's Advertising Standards before launch, especially health, personal attributes and before-and-after content.
  • Watch your customer feedback score and fix shipping or product issues that drive complaints.
  • Check Account Quality every week, so you catch warnings and rejected ads before they build up.

Confirm which of the four problems you have, read the reason in Account Quality, fix the cause, secure your accounts and request a review. Check the Meta Business Help Center for the current steps, since the process changes. Then use the waiting time to fix the setup issues on this list, so the next account you run is harder to restrict. Frequently Asked Questions

Why was my Meta ad account restricted?

Meta restricts ad accounts when its systems flag policy violations, payment problems or suspicious activity. For DTC brands the common causes are health or before-and-after claims, personal attributes wording, failed payments, sudden spend spikes on new accounts and signs of circumvention. Account Quality shows the reason Meta gives.

How do I get my Facebook ad account back?

Open Account Quality, find the disabled account, read the reason, fix the cause and request a review. Meta may ask you to verify your identity or secure your account first. If the cause is an unpaid balance, paying it often restores the account.

What is the difference between a disabled ad account and restricted advertising access?

A disabled ad account affects one ad account. Restricted advertising access affects a person's profile, so they cannot run or manage ads on any account. A business portfolio restriction can affect every ad account inside the portfolio.

How long does a Meta ad account review take?

Meta does not publish a fixed time, and it varies by case. Account Quality shows when a review is in progress and the outcome when it is complete. Check the Meta Business Help Center for current guidance.

Can I just create a new ad account if mine is disabled?

No. Opening new ad accounts, profiles or business portfolios to keep advertising while restricted is treated by Meta as circumventing its systems, and it can lead to permanent restrictions on everything linked to you. Request a review instead.
