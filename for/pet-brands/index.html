What does a pet brand marketing agency do?

A pet brand marketing agency plans and runs paid acquisition and retention for pet products. For DTC pet brands that means emotional UGC starring real pets, vet-informed trust creative, subscription offers for food and treats, Meta and TikTok ads built around those angles, and email flows that turn a first bag into a recurring order.

Winning angles and creative for pet brands

Pet creative lives or dies on the animal. Footage of a real pet reacting to the product (the head tilt, the tail, the empty bowl) does more work than any graphic. The second pillar is owner worry: itchy skin, picky eating, anxiety during storms, joint stiffness in older dogs. The third is trust: who formulated it, what is in it, and why it is different from the bag at the supermarket.

We build each concept into a matrix of angles, hook styles and formats so the account always has fresh variations to test. For pet brands the strongest starting angles are usually these:

  • Reaction hooks: the first two seconds show the pet's response to opening the pack, not the product on a white background.
  • Problem and relief: a scratching dog or a cat ignoring its food, followed by a realistic before and after told by the owner.
  • Ingredient transparency: a flat lay of the actual ingredients next to a competitor-style label, framed as education rather than attack.
  • Vet-informed explainers: a qualified vet or nutritionist on camera explaining what to look for, used only when that person genuinely reviewed the product.
  • Life stage angles: puppy, adult and senior versions of the same ad, because owners self-select quickly.
  • Breed and size call-outs: a French bulldog owner stops for a French bulldog, which keeps hooks specific without targeting anyone by personal attribute.

Offers and funnel for pet food, treats and supplements

Most pet consumables are bought on a cycle, so the funnel should be designed around the second and third order, not just the first. The first-order offer reduces risk (a starter size, a trial bag or a first box discount on subscribe and save) and the product page explains exactly how the subscription works, how to skip and how to cancel. Owners who feel trapped churn and leave reviews about it.

On the product page we look for three things: a feeding or dosing guide by weight so the owner knows how long a bag lasts, real customer photos of pets, and an answer to the transition question (how to switch food without upsetting the stomach). Those details reduce support tickets and refunds as much as they lift conversion rate.

After purchase, the Klaviyo flows carry the brand. A welcome series that covers transition tips, a replenishment reminder timed to the pet's size and the bag's serving count, a review request once the pet has tried it, and a winback for lapsed buyers. Pet owners love sharing photos, so a user-generated content request inside the post-purchase flow also feeds the ad account with new raw footage.

Channel mix for DTC pet brands

Meta is usually the scaling engine because the creative is emotional and the audience is broad. TikTok works well for treats, toys and anything with a visual reaction, and creator content from pet accounts can be run as Spark Ads. Google Shopping and search catch owners who already know what they want, such as a specific formula or a grain-free option.

Pet brand economics and agency pricing

Pet consumables can afford a thinner first order because owners rarely switch once a pet settles on a food. That only holds if you know your real repeat rate. As a worked example: if a first order earns $12 of contribution margin and a subscriber earns $18 on each later order, three reorders turn a break-even acquisition into a profitable customer. We set CAC targets from your numbers, not from a generic ROAS figure.

Agency pricing is quoted after the audit. Common models are a monthly retainer, a percentage of ad spend, a performance fee or a mix. We usually fit pet brands doing roughly $20K to $2M+ a month with product-market fit and at least $10K to $20K a month in paid ads.

Frequently asked questions

Do pet brands do better on Meta or TikTok?

Most pet brands scale on Meta first because emotional pet footage works across a broad audience and the buying journey is easy to track. TikTok can be stronger for treats, toys and grooming products where a reaction is the whole story. We usually prove the offer on Meta, then add TikTok creator content once there is a steady supply of pet videos.

Should a pet food brand push subscriptions from the first order?

Offer subscription on the first order, but make it a clear choice rather than a default the owner discovers later. A small subscribe and save discount, an obvious skip button and easy cancellation reduce churn and chargebacks. Some owners want to test a starter bag first, so keep a one-time option and follow up with a replenishment flow to convert them.

Can pet supplement ads make health claims?

Keep claims to what your product can substantiate, and avoid promising to treat or cure a condition. Meta and TikTok review health-related language closely, and regulators also look at pet supplement marketing. We write around support and comfort language, show real owner experiences without exaggeration, and keep any vet involvement genuine and accurately described in the ad.

Where does pet UGC come from?

The best pet UGC comes from customers, pet creators and your own team's animals. We brief creators with shot lists built around the first two seconds, ask customers for videos in the post-purchase flow, and work with our sister studio SevenFigureStudio when a brand needs produced UGC at volume. Real pets on camera matter more than polished lighting.

What ROAS should a pet brand aim for?

There is no single target. A pet food brand with strong reorders can accept a lower first-order ROAS than a one-off toy brand. Work out your break-even ROAS from contribution margin, then decide how much repeat revenue you are willing to count. Across our 25 published case studies in other niches the median is 3.97x, which is context rather than a pet benchmark.
