Food and Beverage DTC Marketing That Builds Repeat Customers

Home / Industries / Food and Beverage

Selling food online has a problem no other category has: nobody can taste it through a screen. Food and beverage DTC marketing has to make a stranger confident enough to pay for something they have never eaten, plus shipping, and then make sure they order again. Brand Scaling is a performance marketing agency for Shopify brands across 30+ niches, and this page lays out how we think about acquisition, offers and repeat rate for DTC food, drink and coffee brands.

What is food and beverage DTC marketing?

Food and beverage DTC marketing is selling snacks, drinks, coffee, pantry items and similar products directly online. It focuses on getting a first taste through sampling or bundles, beating the taste objection with sensory creative, keeping shipping costs under control, and growing subscriptions and repeat rate so each customer pays back acquisition cost.

Beating the taste objection with creative

The first job of food creative is to make the viewer imagine the taste and texture. The second is to remove the fear of wasting money on something they will not like. The third is to give them a reason to choose you over the supermarket shelf, which is usually an ingredient story, a dietary fit or a format that is hard to find in stores.

  • Sensory close-ups: the crunch, the pour, the steam, the drip. Sound matters, so we design hooks to work with audio on and off.
  • First bite reactions from real customers and creators, unscripted enough to feel honest.
  • Dietary fit call-outs: high protein, low sugar, gluten free, plant based, used only where the claim is accurate and properly substantiated.
  • Ingredient and origin stories: the farm, the roaster, the short ingredient list compared with a typical label.
  • Use occasions: the afternoon slump, the gym bag, the lunchbox, the dinner party, so the product has a moment in the viewer's day.
  • Variety and discovery: flavour packs and mix and match boxes that turn uncertainty into curiosity.

Offers, sampling and the shipping cost problem

Shipping is often the silent killer in food and beverage. Heavy liquids, glass, and anything that needs insulation or cold packs can cost more to ship than the product earns. Before we spend on ads we model landed cost per order by box size and destination, then build offers that make the economics work.

Sampling offers lower the risk of a first order. A variety pack or tasting set lets a new customer find a favourite, which raises the chance of a full-size reorder. Pricing the sampler close to cost can be fine if your data shows those buyers come back, but only if you know that number. Bundles and multi-packs spread shipping across more units and usually belong at the centre of the offer, with a free shipping threshold set where it protects margin.

Subscriptions fit coffee, protein snacks, drinks and pantry staples. We test subscribe and save discounts, flexible delivery intervals and the option to swap flavours, because boredom is a common reason food subscribers cancel. A clear skip and pause option often saves more subscribers than a bigger discount.

Repeat rate is the real growth metric

Food brands rarely win on the first order. The business is built on how many customers come back and how fast. We track repeat rate by cohort and by first product purchased, because the flavour or bundle someone starts with often predicts whether they stay. That insight feeds back into which products we lead with in ads.

Klaviyo email and SMS carry most of the repeat revenue: a welcome series with serving ideas, replenishment reminders timed to how long a pack lasts, new flavour drops, and winbacks for buyers who went quiet. Recipes and usage content keep the brand in the kitchen without discounting every send.

Channel mix for DTC food and drink

Meta is usually the most reliable channel for food and beverage acquisition, with UGC and sensory video doing most of the work. TikTok suits snacks and drinks with a strong visual moment, and TikTok Shop can work for impulse-priced items where creators show the product on camera. Google search and Shopping capture people looking for a specific product, flavour or dietary option. Amazon and retail may sit alongside DTC for many food brands; we focus on the DTC side and keep messaging consistent.

Alcohol and some functional drinks carry extra platform restrictions on age targeting and claims, so we check policy before building a launch plan rather than after the first rejection.

Frequently asked questions

How do you sell food online when people cannot taste it?

Lower the risk and make the taste vivid. Sampler and variety packs let people try several flavours for a modest price, while sensory video with sound, close-ups and honest first bite reactions helps them imagine eating it. Clear ingredient and dietary information answers the practical questions. Then follow up quickly so a good first experience becomes a second order.

How do DTC food brands handle shipping costs?

Model landed cost per order by box size and destination before scaling ads. Then use bundles and multi-packs to spread shipping across more units, set a free shipping threshold that protects margin, and reconsider packaging weight. Some brands limit paid campaigns to regions they can ship to profitably. Your break-even ROAS should always include shipping and packaging.

Are subscriptions worth it for food and beverage brands?

For products people consume on a routine, such as coffee, protein snacks and drinks, subscriptions usually lift lifetime value. Make them flexible: adjustable delivery intervals, easy skipping and the ability to swap flavours. Flavour fatigue is a common reason for cancelling, so rotating new options through email often protects retention better than a deeper subscribe and save discount.

Should a food brand sell on TikTok Shop?

TikTok Shop can suit snacks and drinks with a strong visual moment and an impulse-friendly price, especially when creators show the product on camera. It works less well for heavy or perishable items with high shipping costs. We test it once the core DTC offer is profitable, and keep pricing consistent with the website to avoid undercutting your own store.

What metrics matter most for DTC food brands?

Repeat rate by cohort, contribution margin after shipping, time between orders and subscription churn matter as much as first-order ROAS. A brand with a strong second order can afford a higher acquisition cost than one that sells once. We look at which first product leads to the best repeat behaviour and lead with that product in paid ads.
