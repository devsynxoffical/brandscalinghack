How is wellness brand marketing different from other DTC categories?

Wellness brand marketing has stricter ad policies, more sceptical buyers and a longer path to purchase. It works when creative educates instead of promising outcomes, claims stay compliant and the store builds trust before the sale. Brand Scaling applies the playbook from its supplement and fitness accounts to wellness brands.

Compliance and claims in wellness advertising

Wellness sits close to health, and ad platforms treat it that way. Products that touch sleep, stress, pain, weight or mood attract extra review, and copy that sounds like a medical promise is the fastest way to lose approval. The answer is not vague ads, it is precise ones: say what the product is, how it is used and what it is designed to support, and let education do the persuading.

  • No claims to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition
  • No language that implies the viewer has a problem, such as "your anxiety"
  • Results described as personal experience, not as expected outcomes
  • Ingredient and material claims matched to evidence you can show
  • Landing pages, emails and creator scripts held to the same standard as ads

Winning angles and education-led creative

Wellness buyers want to understand before they buy. The creative that works teaches something useful in the first few seconds, then connects it to the product. We ship 30 to 50+ new creatives a month from a 45+ cell hook and angle matrix, and for wellness the matrix is weighted toward education and ritual formats rather than hard offers.

  • Explainer: one useful idea about sleep, recovery or routine, then the product as the practical step
  • Ritual: the evening wind-down or morning routine the product belongs to
  • Expert voice: a qualified practitioner or founder explaining why the product was made
  • Sensory: texture, scent, sound or feel, which builds desire without claims
  • Honest review: a real customer describing how it fits their week

Offers and funnel: trust before the sale

Because wellness buyers take longer to decide, the funnel needs more than a product page. We often put an education page or advertorial between the ad and the store, use retargeting windows long enough for people to come back, and build product pages that answer the trust questions directly: what is in it, how it is made, who it is not for and what happens if it does not suit you.

Offers lean on low-risk trials, starter kits and subscriptions with easy pausing, since a buyer who feels trapped is a buyer who leaves a bad review.

Trust elements we add to wellness product pages

  • Full ingredient or material list with plain-English explanations
  • Testing, certification or sourcing details where they exist
  • Clear guarantee or returns terms
  • Reviews grouped by use case, not just star rating

Channel mix for wellness brands

Meta is usually the main acquisition channel, using broad campaigns and education creative. TikTok can work for routine and sensory content, although its health policies are strict and vary by market. Google Search is valuable in wellness because people actively research problems like poor sleep, and a well-structured search campaign captures that intent. Email and SMS carry most of the long-term value through education sequences and replenishment.

Which wellness brands we are a good fit for

We are a weaker fit for brands whose growth depends on medical claims, practitioner-only products or services delivered in person.

Frequently asked questions

Do you work with wellness brands that also offer telehealth or services?

Yes, on the ecommerce side. Many health and wellness brands now sell products alongside consultations or telehealth. We run the paid acquisition, creative and Shopify store for the products: supplements, devices, kits and subscriptions. Regulated medical claims and the clinical or telehealth service itself stay with your compliance team, and every ad goes through Meta and TikTok health policy checks.

Do you have case studies for wellness brands?

Not specifically for wellness. Our published results in this area come from supplement and fitness brands, which share the same ad policy limits, trust challenges and repeat purchase economics. Examples include $137,620 in a month at 3.89x ROAS for a supplement brand. We label these as adjacent so you know exactly what the evidence covers.

Why do wellness ads get rejected on Meta?

Most rejections come from health claims, implied conditions or personal attributes. Ads that say a product treats a condition, or that suggest the viewer has a specific problem, are often flagged. We write copy that describes the product and its intended use, keep testimonials to personal experience and review landing pages too, since platforms check them.

What creative works best for wellness products?

Education-led and ritual creative usually beats hard selling. Explaining one useful idea, showing where the product fits in a daily routine, or letting a founder or qualified expert explain the formula builds trust with sceptical buyers. Sensory content about texture, scent or feel also works because it creates desire without making claims.

How long is the buying cycle for wellness brands?

Often longer than for impulse products, because buyers research before trying something that affects how they feel. That is why we use education pages, longer retargeting windows and email sequences that keep teaching after the first visit. Measuring results over a longer window gives a truer picture than judging ads on same-day purchases.

Should wellness brands use influencers or creators?

Yes, carefully. Creators who genuinely use the product make strong education and ritual content, and it can be run as Spark Ads or partnership ads. Every script still needs a claims check, since a creator's words in your ad are treated as your claims. We brief creators on what they can and cannot say.
