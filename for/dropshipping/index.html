What does a dropshipping ads agency actually do?

A dropshipping ads agency tests products and angles quickly, scales winners with broad Meta campaigns and protects margin as spend grows. Brand Scaling runs this for Shopify stores, with results such as $2.3M at 11.6x ROAS in a year and $185,936 in 21 days at a 56% profit margin.

Product testing: finding a winner worth scaling

Most dropshipping stores do not fail on ads, they fail on product. A winning product solves a clear problem, can be shown working in a few seconds of video, has a price that supports a healthy margin after ad costs, and is not already sold on every other store at half the price.

We test products in short, structured rounds: a few creatives per product, a small fixed budget and a clear kill rule based on cost per purchase and margin. Products that pass get more angles and budget. Products that fail get cut quickly so spend moves to the next test.

  • Problem clarity: can a viewer tell what it fixes without sound?
  • Demo-ability: does the product show a visible result in the first three seconds?
  • Margin room: is there enough gap between landed cost and price to pay for ads?
  • Saturation: how many other stores are running the same product and angle?

Niche the store before you scale it

General stores can find a winner, but they struggle to scale it. Buyers trust a store that looks like it specialises in their problem, and Meta's delivery tends to reward a consistent product story. Once a product proves itself, we usually move it to a niched store or rebuild the existing one around it: one category, a clear brand name, product photography that matches, and pages that answer real questions.

Niching also opens up AOV. A store built around one problem can sell the natural add-ons, which turns a single-product store into something with repeat customers and bundle offers.

Winning angles and creative for dropshipping

Creative is the main targeting lever in broad campaigns, so a dropshipping store's results track its creative volume closely. We ship 30 to 50+ new creatives a month from a 45+ cell hook and angle matrix, and we research competitors through the Meta Ads Library to see which angles are already proven in the market.

  • Problem-solution demo filmed on a phone, with the problem shown first
  • Pattern interrupt hooks that stop the scroll without misleading the viewer
  • Comparison against the expensive or awkward alternative
  • UGC reviews and reaction videos from real customers
  • Offer-led creative for peaks like Q4, BFCM and New Year

Broad campaigns, offers and funnel

Our scaling structure for dropshipping is simple on purpose: a small number of broad campaigns, often Advantage+, with creative doing the targeting, and budget raised in steady 20 to 30% steps. A simple funnel (ad to product page to checkout, with a post-purchase upsell) usually beats a complicated one.

Offers lean on bundles and quantity breaks rather than deep discounts, and we watch margin closely, because a store can grow revenue while losing money. One store scaled to $185,936 in 21 days at a 56% profit margin; another hit $148K in a single day on the way to a $294K month at 37% net margin. A Q4 run-up delivered $449,221 in 49 days for a newer client.

Channel mix for dropshipping stores

Meta carries the large majority of spend for most stores we run. TikTok is a strong second channel for products with a visual demo, and TikTok Shop can work for impulse price points. Google Shopping becomes useful once a niched store has branded searches and a clean feed. Email and SMS are often missing entirely in dropshipping, and adding abandoned cart, post-purchase and win-back flows is one of the fastest ways to lift blended margin.

Fix fulfilment before you add budget

Scaling ads on top of weak fulfilment creates chargebacks, refunds and bad reviews that slow the account down later. Before the Scale phase we check the parts of the business that ads cannot fix.

  • Shipping times stated honestly on the product page and in order emails
  • A backup supplier or stock buffer for the winning product
  • Payment processor reserves and chargeback rates watched as volume grows
  • Customer support response times that keep up with order volume

Frequently asked questions

Do you work with brand-new dropshipping stores?

Usually not. We work best with stores that already have sales and a product with some traction, typically spending at least $10K to $20K a month on ads. If you are still searching for a first winner, the audit can still help you decide what to test, but our process is built for scaling, not for starting from zero.

What ROAS do dropshipping stores need to be profitable?

It depends on your margin after product cost, shipping and fees. Our published dropshipping accounts range from 2.53x to 11.6x ROAS. A store with a 60% gross margin breaks even around 1.67x, while one with a 35% margin needs about 2.86x. We calculate your break-even ROAS first, then set targets above it.

How many products should a dropshipping store test?

Enough to find a winner, but not so many that each gets too little budget to judge. We prefer testing a few products properly, each with several creatives and a clear kill rule, over testing dozens with one ad each. A product that fails with three strong angles is rarely saved by a fourth.

Should a dropshipping store use broad targeting or interests?

Broad targeting, in most cases. Meta's delivery system finds buyers based on the creative and conversion data, so interest stacks usually add cost without improving results. We run consolidated broad or Advantage+ campaigns and let creative do the targeting work, which is how most of our published dropshipping accounts scaled.

How do you scale a dropshipping store without losing margin?

Raise budgets in steady 20 to 30% steps, use cost caps once there is enough data, keep creative fresh so frequency does not climb, and lift AOV with bundles and post-purchase upsells. We report profit, not just revenue, because a store can show strong ROAS and still lose money after returns, fees and shipping.

Can you help turn a dropshipping store into a brand?

Yes, and it is often the best way to keep growing. That means niching the store, improving packaging and shipping times, building email and SMS flows and adding related products. Several stores in our case library started as single-product dropshipping stores and grew into DTC brands with repeat customers.
