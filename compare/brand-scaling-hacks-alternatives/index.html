DTC Agency Alternatives: An Honest Guide to Your Options

Home / Services / Agency Alternatives

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

If you are researching dtc agency alternatives, you probably want to know whether a full growth agency like ours is the right call, or whether something lighter or more specialised would serve you better. That is a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends on your stage, budget and bottleneck.

This guide walks through the six main types of help available to a DTC brand, what each is good and bad at, and who it suits. We have also included a section on when Brand Scaling is not the right fit, because a bad match helps nobody.

What are the alternatives to hiring a DTC growth agency?

The main DTC agency alternatives are a freelance media buyer, an in-house team, a specialist PPC agency, a full-service growth agency, a creative-only studio, or a consultant or coaching program. Each fits a different budget, stage and level of internal skill. The right choice depends on what is actually holding your growth back.

Freelancers and in-house teams

Freelance media buyer

A good freelancer can be excellent value. You get direct access to the person doing the work and usually a lower fee than an agency. The risks are capacity and coverage: if they get sick, take on too many clients or leave, your account stalls. Most freelancers focus on campaign management, so you will still need a source of creative, tracking help and store improvements.

In-house team

Building your own team gives you full focus and keeps knowledge inside the business. It also carries the highest fixed cost and the slowest start, because hiring and onboarding take months. It tends to make sense once spend is large and stable, and once someone senior can manage the team. Our agency vs in-house comparison breaks down the cost arithmetic in detail.

Specialist agencies and full-service growth agencies

Specialist PPC agency

Channel specialists, for example a Google Shopping or Amazon-focused agency, often have deeper expertise in that one channel than a generalist. That is valuable if your growth really depends on that channel. The trade-off is a narrow view: they may optimize their channel well while the bigger constraint sits in your creative, offer, landing page or retention.

Full-service growth agency

This is the category Brand Scaling sits in. One team handles paid media, creative strategy and production, tracking, store conversion and retention flows under a single plan. The benefit is that the pieces are joined up: the ads, the product page and the email flows are built to work together. The cost is a higher fee, which only makes sense once you are spending enough on ads for improvements to pay back.

Creative studios and consultants

Creative-only studio

If you already have a strong media buyer and your bottleneck is creative volume, a creative studio can be the most efficient fix. Studios produce UGC, edited video and statics at scale. Our sister studio, SevenFigureStudio, does exactly this for UGC video ads. The limitation is that a studio does not run the ads, read the data or decide what to test next.

Consultant or coaching program

Consultants and coaching programs teach you or your team to run the work. They cost the least and build lasting skill, which suits founders who enjoy the ad account and have the time. The catch is that you still do the execution. If your time is better spent on product, operations or partnerships, coaching can become an expensive way to stay busy.

When Brand Scaling is not the right fit

We work best with DTC brands that already have product-market fit, typically doing about $20K to $2M+ a month and spending at least $10K to $20K a month on paid ads. Outside that range, one of the alternatives above is often a better use of your money.

  • You are pre-launch or have not yet made consistent sales: a consultant or freelancer is usually a better first step.
  • Your ad budget is very small: our fees would take too large a share of spend to pay back.
  • You only need ad creative and already have a strong buyer: a creative studio is more efficient.
  • You only need one channel managed, such as Amazon: a channel specialist will go deeper.
  • You want to learn to run ads yourself: coaching builds your skill more directly.
  • You need guaranteed results by a fixed date: no honest agency can promise that.

How to choose between them

Start with the bottleneck, not the option. If your ads convert but you cannot produce new creative fast enough, the fix is creative. If traffic converts poorly, the fix is the store. If nothing is joined up, you probably need a team that owns the full plan.

Whoever you consider, ask for published results in brands like yours, a clear process and timeline, who exactly will work on your account, and confirmation that you keep ownership of your ad accounts and data. Compare pricing models carefully: retainers, percentage of spend and performance fees shift risk in different ways.

Frequently asked questions

Is a freelancer or an agency better for a DTC brand?

A freelancer is often better value for early brands with modest spend that mainly need campaigns managed. An agency usually fits better once you need creative production, tracking, CRO and retention handled together, and when relying on one person becomes a risk. Judge both on published results in brands similar to yours.

What is the cheapest alternative to a DTC agency?

Consulting or coaching is usually the lowest cash cost, because you do the execution yourself. The real cost is your time. A freelancer is the next step up. Compare options on total cost, including your hours and any creative you must buy separately, rather than on the fee alone.

When should I not hire a growth agency?

Hold off if you have not yet found consistent sales, your ad budget is very small, or your only gap is one narrow task such as creative volume. In those cases a consultant, freelancer or creative studio usually gives a better return. A growth agency pays back best once there is something proven to scale.

Do I need separate agencies for ads and creative?

Not necessarily. Some brands pair a media buyer with a creative studio, which works well when communication is tight. Others prefer one team that owns both, so testing plans and creative briefs come from the same data. The split approach needs someone on your side to coordinate the two.

What should I ask any agency or freelancer before hiring?

Ask for results in brands like yours, a clear process with timelines, who will actually run your account, how much new creative they produce each month, how they report blended results, what the fee includes, and whether you keep full ownership of your ad accounts, pixel and data.
