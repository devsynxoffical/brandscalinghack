Ecommerce Agency vs In House Media Buyer: Which Should You Hire?

Home / Services / Agency vs In-House

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

The ecommerce agency vs in house question usually comes up at the same moment: ads are working, spend is climbing, and the founder can no longer run the account at night. You can hire a media buyer onto payroll, or you can bring in an agency. Both can work. The wrong choice is the one made on the headline cost alone.

This page compares the two on the things that decide the outcome: total cost including tools, creative and your own time, how fast each gets to stable results, which skills you actually get, and when a hybrid makes more sense than either.

Should an ecommerce brand hire an agency or an in-house media buyer?

Most DTC brands spending under roughly $50K a month on ads get more coverage per dollar from an agency, because one fee buys strategy, buying, creative and tracking. In-house wins once spend is large and stable enough to fund a full team and you want daily control. Many brands end up with a hybrid.

The real cost of each option

The most common mistake is comparing an agency fee with a salary. A media buyer on payroll is only one line of what it takes to run paid social well. The other lines are tools, creative production and the hours you or a manager spend directing the work.

The example below uses round, made-up numbers purely to show the arithmetic. Plug in your own salary quotes, tool bills and creative costs.

  • Count management time: an in-house hire still needs direction, reviews and approvals.
  • Count creative: the buyer is only as good as the ads they are given to run.
  • Count tools: attribution, analytics and editing software are often missed in budgets.
  • Ask agencies exactly what the fee includes, especially creative volume and tracking work.

Example arithmetic: in-house (illustrative numbers only)

Assume a media buyer salary of $90,000 a year, plus 20% for payroll taxes and benefits, which gives $108,000. Add $500 a month for analytics, attribution and creative tools ($6,000 a year). Add a creative budget of $4,000 a month for UGC and edited ads ($48,000 a year). Finally, assume the founder spends 5 hours a week managing the hire, valued at $150 an hour, which is about $39,000 a year.

Total in this example: roughly $201,000 a year, or about $16,750 a month. Remove the creative line and it is still about $12,750 a month.

Example arithmetic: agency (illustrative numbers only)

Agencies usually charge a monthly retainer, a percentage of ad spend, a performance fee or a blend. Assume a brand spends $40,000 a month on ads and the agency charges a hypothetical $3,000 retainer plus 10% of spend. That is $7,000 a month. If creative production is included, the comparison is $7,000 against the in-house example of $16,750. If creative is billed separately, add it to both sides.

At $150,000 a month in spend, the same hypothetical fee becomes $18,000 a month, and in-house starts to look cheaper. This is why the answer changes with scale.

Speed to results

An in-house hire has to be found, interviewed, onboarded and given time to learn the account before you can judge them. That often eats a full quarter. If the first hire does not work out, you start again.

An established agency arrives with tested processes. At Brand Scaling, for example, engagements start with a Brand Growth Audit and follow a four-phase plan: Foundation in days 1 to 14 (tracking and Conversions API, offer, margins, product page), Test in days 15 to 45 (weekly creative and a hook and angle matrix), Optimize from day 45 to 90 (conversion rate, AOV, ROAS) and Scale from day 90 onwards. The timeline is the point: you know when to expect a read on performance.

The flip side is that an agency needs time to learn your brand voice and customer, and a good in-house buyer who already knows the product can move faster on day-to-day decisions.

Skills coverage: one person or a team

Scaling a DTC brand on paid social needs more than campaign setup. It needs creative strategy, a steady supply of new ads, clean tracking, a product page that converts, and usually retention flows that turn first orders into repeat revenue. One person rarely covers all of that at a high level.

An agency spreads those skills across specialists. As a reference point, Brand Scaling produces 30 to 50+ new ad creatives and angles a month for partner brands and works across Meta, TikTok, Google, Shopify CRO and Klaviyo. An in-house media buyer can be excellent at buying, but you will likely need a creative producer, an editor and someone for email as well.

When each option makes sense

An agency usually fits when

  • You spend roughly $10K to $100K a month and cannot yet fund a full in-house team.
  • You need creative, tracking and CRO help, not only campaign management.
  • You want results within a quarter rather than after a hiring cycle.
  • You value pattern recognition from many accounts and niches.

In-house usually fits when

  • Ad spend is large and steady enough that percentage fees exceed a team salary.
  • You already have creative production and analytics capacity internally.
  • Your product or compliance needs require constant internal context.
  • You have someone senior who can manage and develop a media buyer.

Hybrid models that work

The choice is not always binary. Plenty of brands combine the two once they pass a certain size, and the split can change over time.

  • Agency strategy plus in-house execution: the agency sets testing plans and reviews the account; your buyer runs it daily.
  • In-house buyer plus outsourced creative: keep buying internal and use a creative studio for UGC and ad volume.
  • Agency for new channels: keep Meta in-house and use an agency to launch TikTok or Google Shopping.
  • Audit then decide: start with an account audit, fix the foundation, then choose who runs it.

Frequently asked questions

Is an agency cheaper than an in-house media buyer?

Often at lower spend levels, yes, because an agency fee can cover buying, creative strategy and tracking that would otherwise need several hires. As ad spend grows, percentage-of-spend fees grow too, and at some point a salaried team becomes cheaper. Run the arithmetic with your own salary quotes, tools and creative costs before deciding.

How do ecommerce agencies usually charge?

The common models are a fixed monthly retainer, a percentage of ad spend, a performance fee tied to revenue or profit, or a mix of these. Each shifts risk differently. Always ask what is included, such as creative volume, tracking setup and reporting, so you compare like with like. Our own pricing is quoted after the Brand Growth Audit.

How long does it take an in-house media buyer to get results?

Expect recruiting, onboarding and learning the account to take a few months before you can judge performance fairly. A strong hire who already knows your category may move faster. The hidden cost is the time lost if the first hire is not the right fit, because the whole cycle restarts.

Can I use an agency and an in-house team together?

Yes, and it is common. Typical setups are an agency setting strategy while an internal buyer executes, an in-house buyer paired with an outsourced creative studio, or an agency launching a new channel such as TikTok while Meta stays internal. The key is clear ownership of the ad account and the testing roadmap.

What should I check before hiring an ecommerce agency?

Look for published results in brands like yours, a clear process with timelines, who will actually work on your account, how much creative they produce each month, and whether you keep ownership of the ad account and data. Ask how they report on blended results, not only platform ROAS.
