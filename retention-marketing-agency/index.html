Ecommerce retention marketing decides how much a customer is worth after the first order, and that number quietly sets how hard you can push paid acquisition. Brand Scaling runs retention as part of the growth system, connected to the same unit economics we use to set ad targets, so a gain in repeat purchase rate becomes room to spend more on Meta and TikTok.

Most DTC brands treat retention as a set of Klaviyo flows that were built once and left alone. We treat it as a measurable program: cohorts first, then the moments in the customer journey where people drop off, then the offers and messages that bring them back.

What is ecommerce retention marketing?

Ecommerce retention marketing is everything a brand does to turn first-time buyers into repeat customers: post-purchase experience, subscriptions, loyalty, win-back campaigns and email and SMS flows. Its goal is higher customer lifetime value, which lets a brand pay more to acquire each new customer through paid ads and still stay profitable.

Why retention lets you afford a higher CAC

If you judge paid ads only on first-order profit, your allowable CAC is capped by one order's contribution margin. Once you know that customers reliably come back, you can judge acquisition on a longer window and bid more for the same customer.

Here is a simple example. A store with a $60 AOV and 50% contribution margin earns $30 on the first order, so a CAC above $30 loses money on day one. If the average customer places a second order within six months at the same margin, contribution over that window rises to $60. The brand can now pay, say, $40 to acquire a customer and still earn a profit over six months, which opens up audiences and bids that were unprofitable before. Retention does not replace acquisition. It changes the maths acquisition is judged by.

  • First-order margin sets the floor for CAC
  • Proven repeat revenue in a fixed window raises the ceiling
  • Cash flow decides how long a payback window you can carry

Start with cohorts, not campaigns

Before building anything, we group customers by the month of their first order and track what each group spends over time. Cohorts show how many customers come back, how fast, and whether recent buyers behave better or worse than older ones. Blended repeat rate hides all of this.

Cohort analysis also shows which first products, offers and channels produce the best long-term customers. A discount-led acquisition campaign may bring in cheap first orders that never return, while a full-price bundle buyer from a different angle comes back three times. That insight goes straight back into the ad account, where we shift budget and creative toward the angles and offers that attract customers who reorder, and away from ones that only win cheap first orders.

The retention levers we work on

Post-purchase experience

The first 30 days after an order decide whether there is a second one. That covers order and shipping updates, how-to-use content, setting expectations for results, and a well-timed replenishment or cross-sell offer. Post-purchase upsells on the thank-you page add revenue to the first order itself.

Subscriptions

For consumable products, subscribe and save turns repeat purchase from a hope into a default. The work is in the offer, the cadence options, making it easy to pause or skip rather than cancel, and messages that keep subscribers engaged.

Loyalty and referral

Points, tiers or simple perks give customers a reason to come back to you instead of a competitor. Referral programs turn happy customers into a low-cost acquisition channel. Both are judged on whether they change behaviour, not on sign-ups.

Win-back

Customers who have passed their expected repurchase window get a sequence that reminds, reassures and, if needed, offers a reason to return. Timing is set from your cohort data, not a generic 90-day rule.

Email and SMS as the delivery system

Most retention runs through email and SMS, which is why this work sits closely with our Klaviyo email and SMS service. The core flows are welcome, browse and cart abandonment, post-purchase, replenishment, win-back and sunset, plus campaigns for launches and seasonal moments. Each flow is mapped to a stage in the customer journey and measured on revenue per recipient and the repeat purchase rate of the people who receive it.

SMS is used where speed matters, such as cart recovery and limited offers, and kept lighter than email so it does not drive opt-outs. List health matters too: suppressing unengaged contacts protects deliverability for everyone else.

How we measure retention

Retention is easy to over-credit, because many repeat customers would have come back anyway. We look at a small set of numbers and track them by cohort over time.

  • Repeat purchase rate at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days
  • Time between first and second order
  • Customer lifetime value over a fixed window, calculated on contribution margin rather than revenue
  • Share of total revenue from returning customers
  • Allowable CAC based on proven LTV, fed back to paid acquisition targets

Frequently asked questions

What is a good repeat purchase rate for ecommerce?

It depends heavily on the product. Consumables such as supplements and skincare should see far more repeat orders than furniture or one-off gifts. Rather than chase an outside benchmark, track your own cohorts at 30, 60, 90 and 180 days and work to improve each new cohort compared with the last.

How do I calculate customer lifetime value?

A simple version is average order value multiplied by the number of orders per customer over a set window, multiplied by contribution margin. Use a fixed window such as 6 or 12 months and real cohort data, not an assumption. Our LTV calculator does the maths and shows the CAC your LTV can support.

Is retention marketing the same as email marketing?

No. Email and SMS are the main delivery channels, but retention also covers the post-purchase experience, subscriptions, loyalty, referral, packaging and how products and offers are chosen. A brand can have strong email flows and still lose customers if the product experience or second-order offer is weak.

When should a DTC brand invest in retention?

Once paid acquisition is running and you have enough customers to see patterns, usually a few thousand buyers. Basic flows such as welcome, abandoned cart and post-purchase should be live from the start. Deeper work on cohorts, subscriptions and loyalty makes sense when acquisition costs start limiting growth.

Should I offer subscriptions for my products?

Subscriptions suit products people use up on a predictable schedule, such as supplements, skincare or coffee. They work best with a clear saving, flexible cadence and easy skip or pause options. For products bought occasionally, a well-timed replenishment or cross-sell flow usually works better than forcing a subscription.

How does retention affect my Meta ads?

Higher proven LTV raises the CAC you can afford, so you can bid more and reach audiences that were unprofitable on first-order margin alone. Cohort data also shows which ads and offers bring in customers who come back, which shapes the next round of creative and targeting decisions.
