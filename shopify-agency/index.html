How to choose a Shopify agency for scaling a DTC brand

Shopify agencies fall into two groups, and DTC brands often hire the wrong one. Design and development studios build beautiful stores and hand them over. Growth agencies treat the store as part of the ad funnel and keep changing it after launch, because the product page that converts cold paid traffic is rarely the one that looks best in a portfolio.

If your traffic comes mainly from Meta and TikTok ads, choose an agency that will commit to numbers after launch, not only to a launch date. Ask these questions on the first call.

  • Will you change the store after launch, and how often do you test product pages, offers and bundles?
  • Do you plan the page around the ads that send traffic to it (the same angle, offer and proof)?
  • Which numbers do you report: conversion rate, revenue per session and AOV by traffic source, or only page speed scores?
  • Who owns the theme, apps and code if we stop working together?
  • Can you show a store you rebuilt and what happened to its conversion rate? Ours: a clinical skincare store reached a 5.25 percent conversion rate in a $61,000 month.

What a store built for paid traffic needs

A store built for organic and returning visitors can look good and still lose money on cold paid traffic. Someone arriving from a 15-second video knows almost nothing about the brand, so the page has to finish the job the ad started. When we build or rebuild a Shopify store for a growth engagement, these are the parts we work on first.

  • A landing page or product page that repeats the ad's promise above the fold.
  • Reviews, before-and-after proof or demonstrations near the add to cart button, not at the bottom of the page.
  • An offer that is easy to understand in a few seconds: one price, one bundle choice, one reason to buy now.
  • A sticky add to cart on mobile and a cart drawer instead of a separate cart page.
  • Post-purchase upsells shown after payment, so they add order value without slowing checkout.
  • Fewer apps, compressed images and lighter scripts so the page loads fast on a phone connection.

The Shopify numbers we track every week

Conversion rate on its own can mislead. A discount can raise it while profit falls, and a higher-priced bundle can lower it while revenue per visitor rises. We read the store numbers together, and always next to the ad account, because a change in traffic quality shows up in the store before it shows up in ROAS.

MetricWhat it tells you
Revenue per sessionThe single best summary of store performance for paid traffic
Conversion rateWhether visitors who arrive go on to buy
Average order valueWhether bundles, quantity breaks and upsells are working
Add to cart rateWhether the product page and offer persuade
Checkout completion rateWhether shipping costs, payment options or surprises lose buyers late
Mobile load timeWhether slow pages are wasting paid clicks
