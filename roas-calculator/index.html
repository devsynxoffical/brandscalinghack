Real ROAS Calculator for Ecommerce

Model your product pricing, product cost (COGS), and target customer acquisition cost (CAC) to calculate break-even ROAS and monthly net profit before scaling paid ad spend.

How do you use a ROAS calculator?

Enter your selling price, your full cost per unit and the cost per purchase you pay or expect to pay. The ROAS calculator returns the ROAS your ads must report to hit that cost, the profit left on each order and a monthly profit estimate. Compare the required ROAS with what your account actually delivers.

The Formulas

ROAS = Total Ad Revenue / Total Ad Spend

Break-Even ROAS = 1 / Gross Margin %

Break-Even ROAS by Product Margin

At 50% gross margin, your break-even ROAS is 2.0x. At 60% gross margin, break-even is 1.67x. At 70% gross margin, break-even is 1.43x.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good ROAS for ecommerce?

There is no universal number. A good ROAS is any figure reliably above your store's break-even ROAS. For most brands with 55% to 75% gross margins, an attributed ROAS of 3.0x to 4.5x represents healthy, sustainable scaling efficiency.

What is the difference between ROAS and MER?

ROAS measures top-line revenue attributed to a single ad platform or campaign. MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio, or blended ROAS) measures total business revenue divided by all marketing spend across Meta, Google, TikTok, and agencies combined.

How do I lower my break-even ROAS?

You can lower your break-even ROAS by improving product gross margins, negotiating lower manufacturing costs, or introducing high-AOV bundles and quantity breaks on your Shopify store.

Is this a real ROAS calculator for ecommerce or an estimate?

It is a real calculator: it uses your actual selling price, product cost, shipping and payment fees and target CAC to return break-even ROAS, profit per order and monthly net profit. The only estimates are the ones you type in, so use real numbers from your Shopify and ad account reports.

What is the best profit optimization tool for DTC brands?

Start with a margin model like this calculator before buying software. Paid profit analytics tools add live order-level data from Shopify and ad platforms, which is worth it above roughly $100K a month in revenue. Below that, a weekly break-even ROAS and contribution margin check covers most decisions.

Worked example: reading the default numbers

The calculator opens with an example product: a $69 retail price, a $14 product cost, a $22 cost per purchase and 30 orders a day. Here is how to read what it returns. These are example numbers, not a benchmark.

Required ROAS is 3.14x, which is $69 divided by $22. It is the ROAS your ad account has to report for a $22 cost per purchase to hold, assuming one unit per order. If Meta or TikTok shows 3.14x or more, you are at or below that cost. Net profit per order is $33: $69 minus $14 minus $22. Monthly figures assume 30 days, so 30 orders a day becomes 900 orders, $62,100 in revenue and $29,700 in net profit, a 47.8% net margin.

Required ROAS is not the same as break-even ROAS. Break-even is the point where profit per order is zero: here $69 divided by the $55 left after product cost, about 1.25x. The gap between 1.25x and 3.14x is your room to spend more per customer before an order stops being profitable.

Why this is a real ROAS calculator, not just revenue divided by spend

A basic ROAS calculator only divides revenue by ad spend, which tells you nothing about profit. A real ROAS calculator for ecommerce starts from what each order costs you: product, shipping, payment fees and the ad cost per purchase. From those it works out your break-even ROAS (the lowest ROAS where an order still pays for itself) and the profit or loss each order leaves at your current cost per purchase.

That is the difference between a dashboard number and a decision. A 3x ROAS can be excellent for a store with a 1.6x break-even and loss-making for a store with a 3.5x break-even. If you only need the break-even point from your margin, the break-even ROAS calculator does that in one step; to plan budget for a revenue goal, use the Facebook ads budget calculator.

ACoS to ROAS: converting Amazon numbers

Amazon reports ACoS (advertising cost of sale) instead of ROAS. ACoS is ad spend divided by ad revenue, so it is simply the inverse of ROAS. To convert, divide 1 by ACoS as a decimal; to go back, divide 1 by ROAS.

ACoSEquivalent ROAS
50%2.0x
40%2.5x
33%3.0x
25%4.0x
20%5.0x

Using it as a Meta ads, Facebook ads, TikTok or dropshipping ROAS calculator

The formula is the same on every channel: ROAS = revenue from ads / ad spend, and the ROAS you need is price / (price - cost per order). What changes is where you read the numbers and what goes into cost.

  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads: compare the result with the Purchase ROAS column in Ads Manager, and with blended MER from Shopify, because Meta can over-credit itself.
  • TikTok ads: use the Complete Payment ROAS column, and check it against MER from Shopify, since in-app attribution often differs from what the store records.
  • Dropshipping stores: include supplier price, shipping to the customer and payment fees in product cost; long shipping times also raise refunds, so add an allowance for them.
  • Amazon: use the ACoS to ROAS table above.

Mistakes that make the result look better than it is

The calculator is only as accurate as the inputs. These are the gaps we see most often when founders model their numbers.

  • Entering only the supplier price as product cost. Add shipping to the customer, packaging, payment processing fees and an allowance for returns.
  • Using list price when most orders carry a discount code. Enter the average price customers actually pay.
  • Using platform ROAS as the truth. Ad platforms can over-credit themselves, so check against blended MER from Shopify revenue and total ad spend.
  • Treating net profit per order as take-home profit. It is before fixed overheads, so the monthly figure still has to cover the rest of the business.
  • Ignoring repeat purchases. If customers reorder, a first order near break-even can still be worth buying. Model lifetime value separately.
