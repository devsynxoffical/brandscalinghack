Ecommerce Conversion Rate: Definition, Formula and Fixes

Home / Glossary / Conversion rate

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

Ecommerce conversion rate is the share of visits to your online store that result in an order. If 30,000 sessions produce 450 orders, your ecommerce conversion rate is 1.5%. It is the bridge between the traffic you pay for and the revenue you keep.

For brands running paid ads, conversion rate is often the most underused lever. Better creative and targeting bring cheaper visitors, but a better store turns every visitor, paid or organic, into more revenue at no extra media cost.

What is ecommerce conversion rate?

Ecommerce conversion rate is the percentage of store sessions that end in a purchase. You calculate it by dividing orders by sessions and multiplying by 100. It measures how well your site turns visitors into buyers, and small improvements have a large effect on revenue, customer acquisition cost and how far you can scale ads.

Conversion rate (Ecommerce conversion rate)
Ecommerce conversion rate is the percentage of store sessions that end in a purchase, calculated as orders divided by sessions.

Formula: Conversion rate = (orders / sessions) x 100

Conversion rate formula and how to calculate it

The formula is conversion rate equals orders divided by sessions, multiplied by 100. Shopify calculates it on sessions, while some analytics tools use users, so be consistent about which you use when comparing periods.

Worked example (illustrative numbers): a store has 30,000 sessions in a month and 450 orders. Conversion rate is 450 divided by 30,000, multiplied by 100, which equals 1.5%.

Now suppose product page and checkout improvements lift conversion rate to 2.0% on the same traffic. Orders rise to 600. At a $70 average order value, that is 150 extra orders and $10,500 more revenue with no change in ad spend. If the brand spent $20,000 on ads, cost per order falls from about $44 to about $33.

It also helps to measure each step of the funnel: product page view to add to cart, add to cart to checkout, and checkout to purchase. That shows where visitors drop off.

What a healthy conversion rate depends on

Conversion rate depends so much on traffic and product that comparing your store with a generic average can mislead you. A store full of cold social traffic will convert lower than one fed by email and branded search, even if the site is identical. Compare against your own history and by traffic source. Key factors include:

  • Traffic source: cold prospecting, retargeting, email and search each convert at different rates.
  • Price point: higher priced products usually take more consideration and convert lower.
  • Device mix: most social traffic is mobile, so mobile experience matters most.
  • Offer and trust: reviews, guarantees and clear shipping terms reduce hesitation.
  • Site speed: slow pages lose paid visitors before they see the product.

How to improve conversion rate

Start where the drop-off is biggest. If many people view products but few add to cart, work on the product page: a clear headline that matches the ad, benefit-led images, social proof near the add to cart button, and answers to the top objections. If carts are abandoned, look at shipping costs, surprise fees and checkout friction.

Speed is a quiet killer. Compress images, remove unused apps and test on a mid-range phone. Make sure the landing page continues the story your ad started, because a mismatch between ad and page loses buyers. Finally, test one change at a time and run it long enough to reach a reliable result. Our CRO work for one clinical skincare store helped it reach a 5.25% store conversion rate in a $61,000 month.

Common conversion rate mistakes

  • Comparing blended conversion rate across months when the traffic mix has changed.
  • Calling A/B tests too early on small samples.
  • Driving all ads to the homepage instead of a focused product or landing page.
  • Fixing design details while ignoring speed, shipping costs or a weak offer.
  • Celebrating a higher conversion rate that came from deep discounts and lower margin.

Related metrics

Conversion rate multiplied by AOV gives revenue per session, a strong single measure of store performance. CPC divided by conversion rate gives cost per purchase, which feeds into CAC. Use the conversion rate calculator to model how much revenue a given lift would add to your store.

When you report conversion rate, split it at least by device and by traffic source. A blended figure can stay flat while mobile conversion falls and email conversion rises, hiding a real problem on the pages your paid traffic lands on. Segmenting also stops you from crediting a site change for an improvement that really came from a shift in traffic mix, such as a big email send.

Frequently asked questions

How is ecommerce conversion rate calculated in Shopify?

Shopify divides the number of sessions that ended in an order by total sessions for the period, then shows it as a percentage. It also breaks the funnel into sessions with an add to cart, sessions that reached checkout and sessions that converted. Those steps help you see whether the problem is the product page, the cart or the checkout.

Why is conversion rate from Meta ads lower than from email?

Email subscribers already know your brand and many have bought before, so they arrive ready to purchase. Meta prospecting reaches people who may be seeing you for the first time. Lower conversion from cold traffic is normal. Judge paid traffic by cost per purchase and CAC, and improve the landing page to help new visitors decide.

What is the quickest way to raise conversion rate?

Look for the largest drop-off first. Common quick wins include speeding up the product page, showing reviews near the add to cart button, stating shipping costs and delivery times clearly, and matching the landing page headline to the ad. Each can go live quickly and often lifts conversion without any change to traffic.

Should I measure conversion rate by sessions or users?

Either works if you stay consistent. Sessions-based conversion rate, which Shopify uses, is lower because one person can visit several times before buying. Users-based conversion rate is higher and closer to the share of people who buy. Pick one definition for reporting and use the same one when comparing periods, channels or tests.

Does conversion rate affect ad performance?

Yes. Ad platforms optimise toward people likely to buy, and a store that converts well sends more purchase signals, which helps the algorithm learn faster. Higher conversion rate also lowers cost per purchase directly, so campaigns that were break-even can become profitable and scale further without any change to the ads themselves.
