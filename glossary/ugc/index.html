What Is UGC? User-Generated Content for DTC Ads

Home / Glossary / UGC

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

What is UGC? UGC stands for user-generated content: photos, videos and reviews made by customers or creators rather than by the brand itself. In paid social, the term now mostly refers to creator videos made specifically for ads, filmed in a natural, phone-shot style that blends into TikTok, Reels and the Facebook feed.

UGC works because it looks and sounds like a recommendation from a person, not an advert from a company. For DTC brands, it is also practical: it is quicker and more flexible to produce than studio content, which makes it ideal for constant creative testing.

UGC is useful for almost any DTC brand selling on social platforms, but it matters most for brands spending enough on Meta or TikTok that ads wear out quickly. It also suits products that are easier to understand when someone demonstrates them on camera.

What is UGC in marketing?

UGC, or user-generated content, is content that shows real people using and talking about a product, often filmed on a phone. In DTC advertising it usually means creator-made videos produced for paid ads. UGC feels native to social feeds, builds trust and gives brands a fast way to test many angles and hooks.

UGC (User-Generated Content)
UGC is content showing real people using or talking about a product, typically creator-made videos used as native-looking ads on social platforms.

How UGC works and how to measure it

There is no formula for UGC itself, but there are clear ways to judge it. Measure each video on hook rate (3-second views divided by impressions), link CTR, cost per purchase and ROAS, and track your hit rate: the share of new UGC ads that beat your current control.

Worked example (illustrative numbers): a brand launches 20 new UGC videos and gives each $250 of test spend, for $5,000 in total. Three of them beat the control ad on cost per purchase. The hit rate is 3 divided by 20, or 15%, and the test cost per winner is $5,000 divided by 3, about $1,667. Tracking these two numbers each month shows whether your briefs and creators are improving.

Common UGC formats include testimonials, unboxings, problem and solution demos, before and after stories, routines and side-by-side comparisons.

What good UGC results depend on

UGC is not a guaranteed win. A poorly briefed video from an unconvincing creator will perform like any other weak ad. What separates effective UGC from content that never scales comes down to a handful of factors:

  • The brief: a clear angle, hook options and proof points to cover.
  • Creator fit: people your customers believe would actually use the product.
  • The first seconds: a strong hook matters more than polish.
  • Real demonstration: showing the product working beats describing it.
  • Volume: enough videos to find the few that become long-running winners.

How to get better results from UGC

Start from angles, not creators. Decide which customer problems, desires and objections you want to test, then brief creators to cover them. Ask for several hooks per video so one recording becomes multiple ads. Our matrix of 5 angles, 3 hook styles and 3 formats turns a small batch of footage into 45 testable combinations.

Edit for performance: add captions, cut dead air and put the most surprising moment first. Whitelisting creator accounts or using TikTok Spark Ads can add social proof, since ads run from the creator's handle. Review results weekly and feed what you learn back into the next brief. Common UGC mistakes

  • Sending creators the product with no brief and hoping for a winning ad.
  • Over-producing UGC until it looks like a polished commercial.
  • Testing one or two videos and concluding UGC does not work.
  • Ignoring usage rights and how long you can run a creator's content in ads.
  • Reusing the same few creators until the audience tires of their faces.

UGC is one of the main defences against creative fatigue, because it can be produced in volume. Its effect shows up first in CTR and hook rate. In automated campaigns such as Advantage+, a varied library of UGC gives Meta more ways to reach different buyers. Our creative strategy agency page explains how we plan UGC alongside other formats.

It also helps to keep a simple creative log. For every UGC video, record the creator, angle, hook style, format, hook rate, CTR and cost per purchase. After a few months, patterns appear: certain angles win repeatedly, some creators consistently outperform, and some hook styles never work for your product. That log turns UGC from a series of guesses into a system your next brief can build on.

Frequently asked questions

What does UGC stand for?

UGC stands for user-generated content. Originally it meant any content created by customers, such as reviews, photos and social posts. In DTC advertising, it now usually refers to videos made by creators, often paid, in a natural phone-filmed style for use in ads on Meta, TikTok and other social platforms.

Is UGC better than studio ads?

Not always, but it often performs well on social platforms because it looks native to the feed and feels like a personal recommendation. Studio content can still work for product detail, premium positioning and brand building. Most DTC brands test both and let cost per purchase decide. The strongest accounts usually mix several formats.

How many UGC videos should I test?

Enough to find a few winners. Most UGC videos will not beat your best ad, so testing a handful rarely tells you much. Brands that scale with UGC usually test new videos every week and track their hit rate. Asking creators for several hooks per video is an efficient way to raise volume without extra shoots.

What should a UGC brief include?

A good brief names the target customer, the angle to test, the key problem and benefit, proof points, several hook options, must-show product moments, things to avoid, the format and length, and the call to action. It should also specify usage rights. Clear briefs produce more usable footage and fewer reshoots.

Can I use customer reviews as UGC ads?

Yes, with permission. Customer photos, videos and written reviews can make strong ad content, especially as social proof. Get explicit consent to use them in paid ads, and check platform rules on testimonials. Many brands combine organic customer content with briefed creator videos to get both authenticity and the angles they want to test.
