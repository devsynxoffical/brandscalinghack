Blended ROAS: What It Is and How to Calculate It

Home / Glossary / Blended ROAS

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

Blended ROAS is your total revenue divided by your total ad spend, across all channels, for the same time period. It is a store-level view of advertising efficiency that ignores how each platform attributes sales.

Platform ROAS figures often overlap. Meta, Google and TikTok can each claim credit for the same order, so adding up their reported revenue frequently gives more than your store actually took in. Blended ROAS avoids that by starting from the revenue your store really recorded.

What is blended ROAS?

Blended ROAS is total store revenue divided by total ad spend across every channel for the same period. Unlike platform ROAS, it does not rely on Meta, Google or TikTok attribution. It shows how efficiently all your advertising works together and is the most reliable top-line number for deciding whether to scale.

Blended ROAS (Blended return on ad spend)
Blended ROAS is total store revenue divided by total ad spend across all channels, measuring overall advertising efficiency without relying on platform attribution.

Formula: Blended ROAS = total revenue / total ad spend

Blended ROAS formula and how to calculate it

The formula is blended ROAS equals total revenue divided by total ad spend. Use net store revenue and add together spend from every paid platform for the same dates.

Worked example (illustrative numbers): in a month, a brand spends $70,000 on Meta and $30,000 on Google, for $100,000 in total. Shopify shows $400,000 of revenue. Blended ROAS is $400,000 divided by $100,000, or 4.0x.

Now look at the platforms. Meta reports $260,000 of revenue, a ROAS of about 3.71x. Google reports $110,000, about 3.67x. Together they claim $370,000, and the remaining $30,000 came from email, organic and direct traffic that ads may have influenced. Blended ROAS captures the whole picture in one number.

Blended ROAS is very close to MER. The usual difference is that MER can include non-ad marketing costs like agency fees and creators, while blended ROAS counts ad spend only.

What a healthy blended ROAS depends on

The right blended ROAS starts with your contribution margin. Divide 1 by your margin before marketing to find break-even, then add room for fixed costs and profit. For reference, the 25 case studies published on our site show a median ROAS of 3.97x, with the middle half between 3.61x and 4.39x and a full range from 2.06x to 11.6x. Those are our own client results, not an industry benchmark, and your target should come from your numbers. Factors include:

  • Contribution margin: higher margins allow a lower blended ROAS.
  • Repeat revenue share: a large returning customer base lifts blended ROAS without new ad efficiency.
  • Growth goals: brands buying market share may run lower blended ROAS on purpose.
  • Seasonality: Q4 and launches move both revenue and costs.
  • Channel mix: search, social and retention channels each contribute differently.

How to improve blended ROAS

Improve the store first. Higher conversion rate and AOV raise revenue from the same ad spend across every channel at once. Then improve the ads: fresh creative, clean tracking through the Conversions API and a sensible campaign structure all lower acquisition costs.

Grow retention so email and SMS bring in revenue without extra ad spend. When scaling, raise budgets in steps, often 20% to 30% at a time, and watch blended ROAS weekly rather than daily. A gentle decline as spend rises is normal, while a sharp drop usually means creative fatigue or saturation.

Also check that each channel earns its place. Pause a small channel for a short, controlled period and watch total revenue. If blended ROAS rises and revenue barely moves, that spend was not adding much.

Common blended ROAS mistakes

  • Adding up platform-reported revenue and calling it blended ROAS.
  • Comparing periods with very different promotions or email sends.
  • Forgetting a channel, such as TikTok or influencer whitelisting, in total spend.
  • Judging blended ROAS without knowing your break-even figure.
  • Ignoring new customer revenue, which can fall while blended ROAS looks stable.

Related metrics

MER is the closest relative and often used interchangeably. Attribution windows explain why platform ROAS differs from blended ROAS. Contribution margin tells you what blended ROAS you need to make money. Our guide to what ROAS is covers platform ROAS in more depth, and the MER calculator works for blended ROAS too.

In practice, the strongest reporting setup uses blended ROAS as the headline, platform ROAS for decisions inside each channel, and new customer revenue as a check on growth. If blended ROAS holds while platform ROAS falls, other channels may be picking up the slack. If platform ROAS rises but blended ROAS does not, platforms are likely claiming sales that would have happened anyway. Reviewing all three each week keeps scaling decisions grounded in what the business actually earned.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between blended ROAS and platform ROAS?

Platform ROAS is calculated by one ad platform using only the sales it attributes to its own ads. Blended ROAS divides all store revenue by all ad spend, with no attribution involved. Platform ROAS helps you optimise within a channel, while blended ROAS tells you whether total advertising is paying off for the business.

Is blended ROAS the same as MER?

They are very similar and many brands use the terms interchangeably. The usual distinction is that blended ROAS divides revenue by ad spend only, while MER can divide by all marketing costs, including agency fees, creator payments and tools. Choose one definition, write it down and use it consistently in reports.

Should blended ROAS include email revenue?

Yes. Blended ROAS uses total store revenue, which includes email, organic and direct sales. That is part of its value: paid ads often drive later purchases through other channels. If you want to isolate paid performance, track new customer revenue or new customer ROAS alongside it, since repeat email revenue can flatter the blended figure.

What blended ROAS do I need to be profitable?

Start from your contribution margin before marketing. Divide 1 by that margin to find break-even blended ROAS. For example, with a 50% margin, break-even is 2.0x. To make a profit after fixed costs, you need to sit above that. The break-even ROAS calculator and MER calculator help you work it out.

Why is my blended ROAS higher than Meta ROAS?

Your store revenue includes sales from email, organic search, direct visits and repeat customers that no ad platform claims. Meta also misses some purchases due to tracking gaps or attribution windows. So total revenue divided by total spend is often higher than any single platform figure, which is why both views are worth tracking.
