What Is CPC? Cost Per Click for Ecommerce Ads

Home / Glossary / CPC

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

What is CPC? CPC stands for cost per click: the average price you pay for one click on your ad. On Google Search you often bid on clicks directly, while on Meta and TikTok you usually pay for impressions and CPC is the result of how well your ad turns those impressions into clicks.

Meta reports two versions. CPC (all) counts every click, including likes, profile taps and "see more". CPC (cost per link click) counts only clicks that leave for your site. For ecommerce, link CPC is the one worth tracking, because a click that never reaches the product page cannot become a sale.

What is CPC in advertising?

CPC, or cost per click, is the average amount you pay each time someone clicks your ad. It equals ad spend divided by clicks. For DTC brands, link CPC is the version that matters, because it shows what each store visit costs and connects directly to conversion rate and customer acquisition cost.

CPC (Cost Per Click)
CPC is the average amount you pay for each click on an ad, calculated as total ad spend divided by the number of clicks.

Formula: CPC = ad spend / clicks

CPC formula and how to calculate it

The formula is CPC equals ad spend divided by number of clicks. Use link clicks for ecommerce so the number reflects real store visits.

Worked example (illustrative numbers): a Meta campaign spends $2,500 and generates 1,875 link clicks. CPC is $2,500 divided by 1,875, which is about $1.33.

CPC is driven by two other metrics. It equals CPM divided by clicks per thousand impressions. In this example CPM is $20 and CTR is 1.5%, so there are 15 clicks per thousand impressions and $20 divided by 15 gives the same $1.33.

Now carry it through to purchases. If 2.5% of those visitors buy, the campaign produces about 47 orders, and cost per purchase is roughly $53. Lift the store conversion rate to 3.5% and the same clicks produce about 66 orders at roughly $38 each. CPC is the same, but acquisition cost drops sharply.

What a healthy CPC depends on

A healthy CPC is the highest click cost that still lets you acquire customers profitably. You can find your ceiling by multiplying your target cost per purchase by your conversion rate. With a $45 target and a 3% conversion rate, you can afford up to $1.35 per click. Above that, every order costs more than you planned. Several factors move CPC:

  • CPM: when impressions get more expensive, clicks usually do too.
  • CTR: a scroll-stopping hook earns more clicks from the same impressions.
  • Market and season: competitive months and high-income countries raise costs.
  • Platform and placement: search, feed, stories and reels each price clicks differently.
  • Offer clarity: ads that make the value obvious attract more motivated clicks.

How to lower CPC

The most reliable way to lower CPC is to raise CTR. That comes down to the first two seconds of a video or the headline of a static ad. Test several hooks for each angle: a problem callout, a bold claim backed by proof, a demonstration, or a customer reaction. Our creative matrix of 5 angles, 3 hook styles and 3 formats gives 45 combinations to test, which is how we find the versions that earn clicks cheaply.

Keep creative fresh, because tired ads see CTR fall and CPC rise. Use broad targeting and let the algorithm find clickers who also buy. Make the call to action and the offer clear so people who click know why. And always check what happens after the click. A lower CPC that brings in browsers rather than buyers does not help.

Common CPC mistakes

  • Tracking CPC (all) instead of link CPC, which flatters results with clicks that never reach your store.
  • Using clickbait hooks that win cheap clicks but attract people with no intent to buy.
  • Optimising campaigns for link clicks rather than purchases, which tells Meta to find clickers, not customers.
  • Judging CPC without conversion rate, so a slightly pricier but higher-intent audience gets cut.
  • Ignoring landing page speed, which wastes paid clicks before the page even loads.

Related metrics

CPC sits between CPM and conversion rate. CPM sets the price of attention, CTR decides how much of that attention turns into visits, and conversion rate decides how many visits become orders. Together they produce cost per purchase and, ultimately, CAC. When planning a test budget, the Meta ads budget calculator helps you model how many clicks and purchases a given spend should buy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CPC and CPC (all) on Meta?

CPC (all) divides spend by every type of click on the ad, including likes, comments, profile visits and expanding text. Cost per link click divides spend only by clicks that go to your website. Link CPC is usually higher but far more useful for ecommerce, because only those clicks can turn into store visits and purchases.

How do I calculate the maximum CPC I can afford?

Multiply your target cost per purchase by your landing page conversion rate. If you can afford $45 per order and 3% of visitors buy, your maximum CPC is $1.35. Anything above that means each order costs more than your target. Improving conversion rate raises the ceiling, which is why store fixes often make ads easier to scale.

Why is my CPC high on Meta?

High CPC usually means low CTR, high CPM or both. Low CTR points to weak hooks or tired creative, while high CPM can come from seasonal competition, a narrow audience or poor engagement. Look at frequency, CTR and CPM on your main ads. Refreshing creative with new angles is often the quickest fix.

Should I optimise Meta campaigns for clicks or purchases?

For ecommerce, optimise for purchases once your pixel and Conversions API are sending reliable purchase data. A link click objective will lower CPC, but Meta will find people who like clicking rather than people who buy. Purchase-optimised campaigns often have a higher CPC yet deliver a lower cost per customer, which is what pays the bills.

Is CPC on TikTok different from Meta?

The formula is the same, but behaviour differs. TikTok users scroll fast and expect native, entertaining video, so hooks matter even more and CTR can vary widely between creatives. Placements, audience habits and auction dynamics also differ. Track link CPC on each platform separately and compare them on cost per purchase rather than CPC alone.
