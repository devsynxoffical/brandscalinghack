What Is CAC? Customer Acquisition Cost Explained

Home / Glossary / CAC

By Gaurav Kapoor, Founder, Brand Scaling · Updated 2026-11-05

What is CAC? CAC stands for customer acquisition cost: the average amount you spend to gain one new paying customer. For a DTC brand, it is the number that decides whether paid growth builds a business or just buys revenue at a loss.

CAC is different from cost per purchase in Ads Manager. Cost per purchase counts every attributed order, including repeat buyers and orders the platform claims but did not cause. CAC uses new customers from your own store data, so it reflects what it really costs to grow your customer base.

What is CAC in ecommerce?

CAC, or customer acquisition cost, is the total amount you spend on marketing to win new customers divided by the number of new customers acquired in that period. It tells a DTC brand what each first-time buyer costs, and it only means something when compared with that customer's margin and lifetime value.

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
CAC is the average marketing cost to acquire one new paying customer, calculated as acquisition spend divided by new customers in the same period.

Formula: CAC = total acquisition spend / number of new customers

CAC formula and how to calculate it

The basic formula is CAC equals total acquisition spend divided by new customers acquired in the same period. Pull new customer counts from Shopify, not from ad platforms, and decide whether acquisition spend includes only media or also creative production, agency fees and influencer costs. Including them gives you a fully loaded CAC.

Worked example (illustrative numbers): a brand spends $60,000 on paid ads in a month and Shopify records 1,200 first-time customers. CAC is $60,000 divided by 1,200, which equals $50.

Now check payback. If the average first order is $80 and gross margin is 60%, the first order produces $48 of gross profit. With a $50 CAC, the brand is $2 short on the first order and needs a second purchase to turn a profit on that customer. That single comparison tells you more than CAC on its own.

What a healthy CAC depends on

A healthy CAC is set by what a customer is worth to you, not by a number another brand reports. Two stores in the same niche can have very different safe CAC levels.

A practical way to set a ceiling is to start from first-order contribution margin, then decide how many orders you are willing to wait for payback. A brand with $48 of first-order gross profit and confident repeat data might accept a $70 CAC because the second order covers the gap. A brand with weak retention should keep CAC close to that first-order profit. The factors below move that ceiling up or down.

  • First-order contribution margin: how much profit the first purchase leaves after product, shipping and fees.
  • Lifetime value: brands with consumables and strong retention can afford a higher CAC.
  • Cash position: a brand with limited cash needs faster payback and therefore a lower CAC.
  • Price point and AOV: higher priced products naturally carry higher acquisition costs.
  • Channel mix: prospecting on cold audiences costs more per customer than branded search.

How to lower CAC

Most CAC gains come from three places. First, creative: new hooks and angles that stop the scroll lower CPM and CPC, which lowers what you pay for each buyer. Testing 30 to 50 new creatives a month is how we keep acquisition costs from drifting up on partner brands.

Second, conversion rate. If the product page converts better, the same traffic produces more customers, and CAC falls without touching the ads. Speed, clearer offers, reviews and bundles all play a part.

Third, signal quality. A clean Conversions API setup and a focus on new customer purchases help Meta find buyers instead of recycling people who already bought. For a deeper playbook, see our guide on how to lower CAC.

Common CAC mistakes

  • Using platform cost per purchase as CAC, which mixes repeat orders and over-attributed sales.
  • Leaving out creative and agency costs, then wondering why the business is less profitable than CAC suggests.
  • Judging CAC without LTV or first-order margin, so a "high" CAC gets cut even when customers are very profitable.
  • Averaging CAC across channels and hiding one channel that loses money.
  • Looking at one week and reacting to normal volatility.

Related metrics

CAC is half of the LTV to CAC ratio, the most common test of whether acquisition is sustainable. Average order value and contribution margin decide how much of CAC the first order pays back. MER shows the same efficiency question at the whole-business level. Use the CAC calculator to work out your own figure and payback period.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CAC and CPA?

CPA, or cost per acquisition, usually means cost per conversion in an ad platform, and that conversion can be any purchase, including repeat buyers. CAC counts only new customers and is normally calculated from your store data. CPA is useful for optimising campaigns day to day, while CAC is the number you use to judge whether growth is profitable.

Should CAC include organic customers?

There are two useful versions. Blended CAC divides all marketing spend by all new customers, including those from organic and word of mouth. Paid CAC tries to isolate customers from paid channels. Blended CAC is simpler and harder to game, so most DTC brands track it as the main number and use paid CAC as a supporting view.

What is a good LTV to CAC ratio?

Many operators aim for lifetime value comfortably above CAC, but the right ratio depends on how quickly you need payback and how reliable your LTV estimate is. Calculate LTV on gross margin, not revenue, and use a realistic time window such as 12 months. If customers pay back within the first or second order, you have more room to scale.

Why is my CAC rising?

Common causes are creative fatigue, rising CPMs in busy seasons, audience saturation as you scale, a drop in site conversion rate, or tracking gaps that push Meta toward weaker buyers. Check frequency and CTR on your top ads first, then landing page conversion rate. Often a fresh batch of creative angles brings CAC back down faster than any targeting change.

How do I calculate CAC payback period?

Divide CAC by the gross profit a customer generates per month or per order. For example, if CAC is $50 and the first order leaves $48 of gross profit, payback takes slightly more than one order. Brands with fast payback can reinvest cash sooner, which is why payback matters as much as the CAC number itself.
