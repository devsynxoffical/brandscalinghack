What is ecommerce email marketing?

Ecommerce email marketing is the automated flows and regular campaigns an online store sends to subscribers and customers: welcome emails, abandoned cart reminders, post-purchase follow-ups, win-back offers and product launches. Done well, it turns customers you already paid to acquire into repeat buyers, which raises lifetime value and lets your ads afford a higher cost per customer.

The email flows every ecommerce store needs

Flows are automated emails triggered by what a shopper does. They are the first thing we build, in this order, because each one recovers or creates sales without new ad spend.

Welcome series

For new subscribers: who you are, why the product works, social proof and a reason to buy now. It sets the tone for every email that follows.

Browse abandonment

For shoppers who viewed a product but did not add it to cart: the product they looked at, answers to common objections and reviews.

Abandoned cart and checkout

For shoppers who started to buy and stopped: a reminder, then proof and help, timed over a few days rather than all at once.

Post-purchase

After the first order: delivery updates, how to use the product, a review request and the next product that fits, so the first order leads to a second.

Replenishment and win-back

For consumables, a reminder when the customer is likely to run out. For lapsed customers, a reason to come back before they forget the brand.

VIP and loyalty

For repeat buyers: early access, bundles and rewards that keep your best customers buying from you instead of a competitor.

Campaigns vs flows

Flows run automatically on behaviour; campaigns are one-off sends to a segment, such as a launch, a sale or a story. You need both, but in a different balance than most brands use.

Growing the list the right way

A bigger list only helps if the people on it want to hear from you. Pop-ups and sign-up forms are set up to collect real addresses and proper SMS consent, with an offer that fits your margin, rather than filling the list with people who never open.

Paid ads feed the list too: every visitor your Meta and TikTok ads bring in is a chance to collect an email, so the welcome flow is planned with the ad creative.

Deliverability: making sure emails reach the inbox

Since February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require bulk senders to authenticate their domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, offer one-click unsubscribe and keep spam complaints low. Stores that miss these see more of their email land in spam or get rejected.

  • Send from your own domain, authenticated with SPF, DKIM and DMARC.
  • Include one-click unsubscribe in every marketing email.
  • Mail engaged subscribers more often and sunset people who never open.
  • Warm up a new sending domain gradually instead of mailing the whole list on day one.
  • Watch spam complaints and bounces after every campaign.

Why email makes your ads more profitable

Ads pay for the first order; email earns the rest. If repeat purchases raise what a customer is worth over a year, you can pay more to acquire each one and still make money, which lets your ad accounts scale further than a competitor who relies on first orders alone.

That is why we plan email and paid ads together: the ad creative, the offer and the welcome flow tell one story, and we judge both on lifetime value and contribution margin, not on opens and clicks.

Frequently asked questions

Which email flows matter most for ecommerce?

Start with the welcome series, abandoned cart and checkout, and post-purchase flows, because they trigger on the moments with the highest intent. Then add browse abandonment, replenishment or win-back, and a VIP flow for repeat customers.

How often should an ecommerce store send email campaigns?

It depends on how engaged your list is and how often you have something worth saying. Many stores send weekly; the right answer is the most often you can send while opens stay healthy and unsubscribes and spam complaints stay low. Engaged segments can hear from you more often than the whole list.

Is Klaviyo the best email platform for Shopify?

Klaviyo is the platform we use for Shopify brands because it syncs Shopify orders, products and browsing data, which the best flows depend on. Our Klaviyo agency page covers how we set it up and run it.

Do you also run SMS marketing?

Yes. SMS is built alongside email in Klaviyo, with proper consent collection, and used for the moments where speed matters, such as cart reminders and launches.
