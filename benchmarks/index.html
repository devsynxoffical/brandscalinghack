What about average ROAS for UK ecommerce?

We have one published UK result, a skincare brand that reached EUR 118,000 in its first month, and that post does not state a ROAS, so we do not publish a UK average. UK brands price with 20 percent VAT included, so calculate ROAS and break-even on revenue excluding VAT, or the number will look about a fifth better than it is. How the benchmarks are built

We only use figures that appear in a published case study with a screenshot of the ad account or store. Where a case shows revenue and spend, we calculate ROAS from those two numbers. Cases without both numbers are left out rather than estimated.

The sample is our own client work, so it reflects brands that chose to scale with an agency and accounts that went well enough to publish. Treat the numbers as what good looks like for brands like these, not as an industry average.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROAS for an ecommerce brand?

It depends on your margin. A good ROAS is one comfortably above your break-even ROAS, which is 1 divided by your contribution margin before ads. In our published case studies the median is 3.97x, but a brand with 70% margins can be very profitable at 2x while a 30% margin brand loses money below 3.3x.

Why not use industry-wide benchmark reports?

Many reports mix brands of every size, margin and attribution setup, and some cannot be checked at all. We prefer a smaller set of numbers we can prove with screenshots. When you compare yourself with any benchmark, compare against your own break-even ROAS and MER trend first.

Will you publish CPM, conversion rate and CAC benchmarks?

Yes, once we have enough accounts measured the same way to publish them honestly. Until then, use the free conversion rate, CAC and Meta ads budget calculators to work from your own data, which is more useful for decisions than any average.
