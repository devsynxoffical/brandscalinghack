GBP, VAT-inclusive prices and what they do to your margin

UK shoppers expect the price on the ad and the product page to include VAT, and the standard rate is 20 percent. That sounds obvious, but it changes the maths in a way American founders often miss. A £60 product is not £60 of revenue: one sixth of it is VAT, so the business keeps £50 before product cost, shipping and ad spend.

We build break-even ROAS for UK accounts on the ex-VAT figure, because the purchase value sent to Meta usually includes VAT. If you are a US brand selling into the UK, the rules for low-value parcels mean VAT is usually collected at checkout rather than at the door, and pricing in GBP with VAT already included avoids the surprise fees that kill repeat orders.

  • Ads and product pages priced in GBP with VAT included
  • Break-even ROAS calculated on revenue after VAT, not the platform figure
  • Bundle and free-shipping thresholds set in round GBP amounts
  • Separate UK campaigns when the margin structure differs from your US store

UK GDPR, PECR and the effect on tracking

Under UK GDPR and PECR, non-essential cookies such as advertising pixels need the visitor's consent before they fire. A proper consent banner means some visitors decline, and their browsing never reaches the Meta pixel or Google tags. Platforms therefore see fewer conversions than actually happened.

This is why the Foundation phase for UK accounts focuses on the Conversions API and consent setup together. Server-side events sent with consent signals respected give the algorithm cleaner data than a browser pixel alone, and Google's consent mode lets modelled conversions fill part of the gap. We judge performance on blended numbers from Shopify, such as MER, so a consent-driven drop in reported conversions is not mistaken for a drop in sales.

Delivery expectations and the UK retail calendar

UK shoppers are used to fast, cheap domestic delivery through Royal Mail tracked services and courier networks, and next-day options are common. An ad that wins the click can still lose the sale if checkout shows a week of shipping from overseas. For UK brands we put the delivery promise into the creative and the product page, and for US brands entering the UK we look at whether stock should sit with a UK fulfilment partner first.

The calendar matters too. Black Friday and Cyber Monday are firmly established in the UK, so BFCM planning starts in October as it does in the US. The difference is Boxing Day on 26 December, a major sales day that American calendars ignore. We plan a separate post-Christmas push with its own offers and creative rather than letting the account go quiet after Christmas.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have an office in London?

No. Our only office is in New York, at 27 W 24th Street, Suite 702. We work with London and UK brands remotely, with live calls in the London afternoon when New York starts its day. We do not have a London team or hold meetings there, and we would rather say that plainly than imply otherwise.

That is the only UK result we have published, so for other categories we point to our agency-wide numbers and the closest relevant case rather than claiming UK results we cannot show.

How does UK cookie consent affect Meta ads performance?

Visitors who decline advertising cookies are not tracked by the browser pixel, so Meta reports fewer purchases than Shopify records. The fix is a correct consent setup plus the Conversions API, which sends server-side events in line with consent choices. We then judge the account on blended revenue and MER rather than on platform-reported ROAS alone.

Should a US brand selling into the UK run separate campaigns?

Usually yes, once UK sales are meaningful. VAT-inclusive GBP pricing, different shipping costs and a different retail calendar change the break-even ROAS, so mixing UK and US buyers in one campaign hides which market is profitable. We often start with a UK test campaign, then split budgets properly once the numbers justify it.

Do you plan Boxing Day campaigns as well as Black Friday?

Yes. UK brands get a BFCM plan that starts in October, then a separate Boxing Day and January plan with its own offer and creative. Many accounts treat 26 December as an afterthought, but UK shoppers expect sales that week, so we brief the creative in advance and keep budgets ready instead of pausing after Christmas.
