Bilingual creative for US Hispanic audiences

Many US Hispanic shoppers are bilingual and move between English and Spanish depending on the platform and the moment, so there is no single right language. The useful question is which version sells better for your product, and that is something to test rather than assume.

We treat Spanish creative as its own set of angles, not a translation of the English ads. A hook that works in English can fall flat when translated word for word, so we brief native-speaking creators and write the script in Spanish from the start. Meta does not allow targeting by ethnicity, so language versions, creative and the audience's response do the work instead.

  • English, Spanish and mixed-language versions of the same angle tested side by side
  • Scripts written natively in Spanish, not machine translated
  • Product pages and checkout copy that match the language of the ad
  • Email and SMS flows with a language preference captured at signup

Shipping into Latin America

Selling from the US into Latin America is a different business from selling domestically. Each country has its own customs process, import duties and taxes, and delivery can take longer with more points of failure. The biggest conversion risk is a surprise charge at the door, so the choice between collecting duties at checkout (DDP) or leaving them to the buyer (DDU) matters for both conversion rate and returns.

Before we spend on ads in a new country we check the basics: landed cost, delivery times, returns and payment methods. Shopify Markets can show local currency and handle duties for some setups. If the numbers do not work, we say so and keep spend in the US until they do.

Frequently asked questions

Do you have an office in Miami?

No. Our only office is in New York, at 27 W 24th Street, Suite 702. We serve Miami brands remotely, and because both cities are on Eastern time, calls and reporting happen in the same working hours. We do not have a local team or hold meetings in Miami, and we would rather say so clearly before you book an audit.

Should a Miami brand run ads in English or Spanish?

Often both, and the answer comes from testing. Many US Hispanic shoppers are bilingual, so we run English, Spanish and mixed-language versions of the same angle and let purchases decide. Spanish ads should be written natively by Spanish speakers rather than translated, and the product page should match the language of the ad that sent the visitor.

Can you target Hispanic audiences on Meta?

Not by ethnicity. Meta does not offer ethnicity-based targeting, so we rely on language, location, creative and broad targeting that learns from who buys. Spanish-language creative with native speakers tends to find its audience through the algorithm. We measure results by purchases and contribution margin, not by assumptions about who is seeing the ads.

Is it worth selling into Latin America from Miami?

It can be, but only once landed cost works. Duties, import taxes, delivery times, returns and payment methods vary by country, and any of them can erase the margin. We check those numbers before spending on ads in a new country. If they do not work, we keep budget focused on the US until they do.

What does a Miami brand need to start working with you?

A DTC brand on Shopify with product-market fit, roughly $20K to $2M+ a month in revenue and at least $10K to $20K a month in ad spend is the usual fit. The first step is a Brand Growth Audit on a video call with Gaurav, covering your ad account, creative, store and unit economics, with a list of what to fix first.
