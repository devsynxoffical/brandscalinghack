Google Shopping Ads Agency for DTC Brands Running Paid Social

Brand Scaling runs Google as a Google Shopping ads agency for DTC brands whose growth is led by Meta and TikTok. Paid social creates demand. People see an ad, then search for the product or the brand on Google and buy. Shopping and Performance Max are where much of that demand gets captured, and where a lot of budget quietly goes to waste when the feed is weak or branded searches are mixed in with real prospecting.

Our job on Google is narrow and practical: a clean product feed, a campaign structure that tells you what is new revenue and what is not, and a return target that comes from your margin rather than from a default setting.

What does a Google Shopping ads agency do?

A Google Shopping ads agency builds and maintains your Merchant Center product feed, runs Shopping and Performance Max campaigns, separates branded from non-branded demand, and sets target ROAS from your margins. For DTC brands it also makes sure Google captures the search demand that Meta and TikTok create without taking credit for it twice.

Start with the Merchant Center feed

Shopping ads are built from your product feed, not from ad copy. Google reads your titles, descriptions, images, prices, product types and custom labels to decide which searches your products appear for. A feed pushed straight from Shopify with default titles usually leaves a lot on the table.

We fix the feed before we touch bids.

  • Titles rewritten with the words people actually search: product type, key attribute, size or count, then brand where it helps.
  • Images checked for policy and clarity, with variant images mapped correctly.
  • Product types and Google product categories set so similar items group together.
  • Custom labels added for margin tier, bestseller status, price band and seasonality, so campaigns can be split by profitability.
  • Disapprovals, price mismatches and shipping errors cleared in Merchant Center.
  • Promotions and product ratings connected where you qualify.

Brand vs non-brand: the split that changes everything

If someone searches your brand name, they already know you, often because of a Meta or TikTok ad. Shopping and Performance Max will happily serve on those searches and report a very high ROAS. That number is real revenue but it is not new demand, and it can hide the fact that non-branded campaigns are losing money.

We separate the two. Branded search gets a modest budget to protect your name from competitors and resellers. Non-branded Shopping and Performance Max, the searches for the product category, get judged on their own ROAS and on new-customer acquisition. Where the account allows, we use brand exclusions on Performance Max and negative keywords on Standard Shopping so the reporting stays honest.

This matters for budget decisions across channels. If Google takes credit for sales Meta created, you might cut the Meta spend that was feeding it.

Performance Max and Standard Shopping

Performance Max runs across Shopping, Search, YouTube, Display, Discover and Gmail from one campaign. It can scale well with a strong feed and good conversion data, but it gives less control and less visibility. Standard Shopping gives you search term data and tighter control over what you bid on.

For most DTC brands we run a mix: Performance Max segmented by margin tier or product group using custom labels, plus Standard Shopping for hero products or categories where we want search-term control. Asset groups get real creative, often cut from the same UGC and product videos that work on Meta, rather than auto-generated assets.

Setting target ROAS from your margins

Target ROAS should come from contribution margin, not a round number. The break-even ROAS on a product is 1 divided by its contribution margin percentage. As a worked example: a product with 50% margin after product cost, shipping and fees breaks even at 2.0x, while one at 35% margin needs about 2.86x just to break even.

We set targets per margin tier so high-margin products can bid more aggressively and low-margin ones are held to a stricter return. Because branded traffic is split out, these targets apply to real prospecting, which is where the profit question actually sits. You can work out your own number with our break-even ROAS calculator at /tools/break-even-roas-calculator.

How Google works alongside Meta and TikTok

Most of our partners spend the majority of their paid budget on Meta, and Google acts as the capture layer. We read the two together. When a new Meta angle starts scaling, branded and category searches often rise, and Shopping should be ready for it with stock, feed and budget.

We report blended MER and new-customer CAC across all channels weekly, so you can see whether Google spend is adding customers or mostly collecting ones you already paid for elsewhere. If you are deciding how to split budget, our comparison at /compare/meta-ads-vs-google-ads covers the trade-offs.

Common problems we find in DTC Google accounts

Most Google accounts we audit for paid social brands have the same handful of issues. None of them need a bigger budget to fix, and several can be cleared in the first two weeks.

  • Branded and non-branded searches in one campaign, so the reported ROAS looks healthy while prospecting loses money.
  • Conversion tracking counting more than purchases, such as add to carts or page views, which teaches bidding to chase the wrong action.
  • One target ROAS across every product, regardless of margin, so low-margin items soak up budget.
  • Products with disapprovals or out-of-stock variants still eating impressions and clicks.
  • Performance Max running on auto-generated assets with no real video, which weakens YouTube and Display placements.
  • No custom labels, so there is no way to separate bestsellers from slow movers or new launches from clearance stock.
  • Google reporting compared only against itself instead of against Shopify orders and blended MER.

Frequently asked questions

Is Performance Max better than Standard Shopping for ecommerce?

Neither wins every time. Performance Max can scale further with a strong feed and good conversion data, but it hides search terms and mixes in branded traffic. Standard Shopping gives more control. Most DTC accounts we see do best with a mix, segmented by margin or product group, with branded searches kept separate.

Why is my Google Shopping ROAS high but profit low?

Usually because branded searches are inside the same campaigns. People who already know your brand convert at a very high rate, which lifts reported ROAS while non-branded prospecting loses money. Splitting brand from non-brand, and setting target ROAS by margin tier, normally shows where the profit actually sits.

How do I optimise my Merchant Center product feed?

Rewrite titles with the terms people search for, starting with product type and key attributes. Fix image and price errors, set accurate product categories, and add custom labels for margin, bestsellers and price bands. Clear disapprovals regularly. A better feed improves which searches you show for before any bid change.

Should DTC brands run Google Shopping if Meta is working?

Usually yes, as a capture channel. Meta and TikTok ads create searches for your product and brand. If you are not on Shopping, competitors and resellers can take those buyers. The key is to measure Google on new customers and blended MER so it does not take credit for demand paid social created.

What target ROAS should I set on Google Shopping?

Start from break-even: 1 divided by your contribution margin percentage. A product with 40% margin breaks even at 2.5x. Set your target above break-even for the profit you want, and use different targets per margin tier. Avoid one account-wide target, which over-bids on low-margin products and under-bids on high-margin ones.
